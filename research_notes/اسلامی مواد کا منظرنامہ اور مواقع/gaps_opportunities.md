*Method note for the report writer: the page-fetch tool was blocked in this session (the egress proxy refused connections to yaqeeninstitute.org, thequint.com, wikipedia and others). ## 1. Which contemporary questions and anxieties dominate among young Muslims?

### Takeaway
Young Muslims, in Pakistan and the diaspora alike, remain highly religious on paper. Pew finds roughly 99% retention among people raised Muslim. The live pressure points sit inside belief rather than in formal exit: doubt driven by ethics, trauma and the problem of evil; hadith scepticism and Quranism; halal money (crypto and riba loans); mental health; AI as a new "mufti"; and identity shocks after Gaza. Pakistan adds a hidden, mostly urban and educated layer of scepticism that cannot be voiced openly because of blasphemy-law risk.

### Cited Findings
**Retention and religiosity (macro picture)**
- About 10% of adults under 55 worldwide who were raised in a religion have switched. Hindus and Muslims have the highest retention: about 99% of those raised Muslim still identify as Muslim — [Pew Research, June 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/26/globally-1-in-10-adults-under-55-have-left-their-childhood-religion/)
- Across 13 countries, 3% or fewer of adults have left or entered Islam. Pew found little variation in Muslim retention except in the US — [Pew Research, March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/03/26/religious-switching-into-and-out-of-islam/)
- Pakistan: a Gallup International survey released in July 2025 reportedly found 94% of Pakistanis religious, about 1% "convinced atheists" and 3% not religious — [EUAA country report (2026)](https://www.euaa.europa.eu/print/pdf/node/31472). Other figures differ: a 2024 Gallup survey is cited as 5% "not religious" and 1% atheist ([Wikipedia: Irreligion in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_Pakistan)), and a World Population Review table lists 4% atheists for 2025 ([WPR](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/most-atheist-countries)). The sources conflict, and none reports a youth breakdown.
- EUAA reports that scepticism, atheism and agnosticism are "increasingly visible particularly among urban, educated youth", above all in universities and digital and social-media spaces. Many such people conceal their views because open atheism intersects with blasphemy laws — [EUAA](https://www.euaa.europa.eu/print/pdf/node/31472)
- A UMT phenomenological study describes a growing number of Pakistani young adults leaving Islam, a subject long treated as taboo — [UMT eScholar](https://escholar.umt.edu.pk/items/c4115c50-1f33-47d8-b325-57f57c6dd264/full). An Urdu-language journal paper studies "the rise of secular trends among youth in Pakistan: causes, manifestations and social effects" — [Al-Irfan, MUL](https://ojs.mul.edu.pk/index.php/alirfan/article/view/1058)
- A Facebook group, "Pakistani Atheists and Agnostics", had 1,100+ members. Its founder says that for every member, two more people message him wanting to join but are afraid — [Tablet Magazine](https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/news/articles/pakistans-secular-muslims) **[date unclear, likely older]**
- A PIPS survey of Pakistani postgraduate students found that 92.4% said religion is important in their life, yet 51.7% do not pray regularly — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/article/1342/what-is-young-pakistan-thinking) **[older, date not shown]**
- The British Council "Next Generation Pakistan" study (3,446 respondents aged 16–34, plus 18 focus groups) found young Pakistanis divided on identity, with religion coming before nationality for many — [British Council, 2023](https://www.britishcouncil.org/research-policy-insight/research-reports/next-generation-pakistan) **[older, 2023]**
- Pulse's study of 2,200 Gen Z respondents in 38 Pakistani cities describes them as religiously observant and tolerant, for example accepting non-Muslim neighbours — [Aurora/Dawn](https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1145404/digitally-pakistani); [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/turns-out-pakistani-gen-z-is-the-most-desi-of-us-all/) **[2023 data]**
- UK: in a 2023 poll of 1,002 Muslims aged 16–24, 89% prayed at home, 75% went to a mosque regularly, and 81% said religious leaders influence their lives (38% of non-Muslims said so) — [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/uk-gen-z-muslims-similar-experiences-except-religiosity); [Church Times](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2023/21-april/news/uk/survey-finds-gen-z-muslims-more-religious-than-non-muslim-peers) **[older, 2023; no 2025 equivalent found]**
- US: ISPU's American Muslim Poll 2025 (NORC, April–May 2025) found Muslims are the youngest US faith community. About a quarter are aged 18–29, against 5–15% in other groups — [ISPU 2025 report](https://ispu.org/american-muslim-poll-2025-full-report-1/); [ISPU "Distress and Disconnection", May 2026](https://ispu.org/?p=36495)

**Doubt: sources and themes**
- An early Yaqeen "Doubt Survey" found that many people's doubts stemmed from traumatic experiences, which led Yaqeen to launch a Trauma series — [Yaqeen, Omar Suleiman lecture](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/lecture/addressing-the-topic-of-trauma-and-faith); see also [Modern Pathways to Doubt in Islam](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/amp/read/paper/modern-pathways-to-doubt-in-islam) **[older, ~2016]**
- A critique of Yaqeen's data argues that ethical and moral objections dominate doubt, and that hadith criticism often rests on modern ethical assumptions — [Islamic Discourse (Substack), opinion](https://islamicdiscourse.substack.com/p/is-ashari-kalam-essential-to-retaining)
- North American research (Jeffrey Lang's work) describes young Muslims and converts with theological, rational and hadith-sceptical doubts who are "unlikely to get any help from the local imam" — [AJIS review](https://ajis.org/index.php/ajiss/article/download/1673/938/2607) **[older]**
- The Nadwi–Akhtar debate (see Q3) centred on the argument from contingency against the problem of evil and free will, which shows which atheism questions resonate in Hindi/Urdu space — [Wikipedia: Shamail Nadwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamail_Nadwi)

**Hadith scepticism and Quranism**
- A University of Malaya thesis (2023) surveyed 197 students in Punjab. Their *knowledge* of anti-hadith ideologies was "moderately low" but their *attitudes* towards these movements were "moderately high". The author attributes this to weak Islamic education and the influence of social media — [UM thesis record](https://cendekia.unisza.edu.my/neuaxis-e/Record/um-14412) **[2023]**
- An Urdu journal paper on the history and drivers of hadith rejection — [Al-Amir](https://alamir.com.pk/index.php/ojs/article/view/232)

**Halal money: crypto and riba**
- In June/July 2026 a Darul Uloom Karachi fatwa signed by Mufti Taqi Usmani held that cryptocurrencies, including USDT and stablecoins, do not qualify as *maal* — [Profit/Pakistan Today, 10 Jul 2026](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/07/10/mufti-taqi-usmani-issues-fatwa-declaring-cryptocurrency-purchases-impermissible-under-shariah); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/407969/crypto-fatwa-sparks-debate-over-pakistans-digital-asset-framework). Reports differ on whether it was issued in June or July.
- Other clerics disagreed. Saylani's mufti argued for conditional permissibility and sent his fatwa to the CII and the State Bank, and another cleric publicly called crypto permissible — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/crypto/pakistan-clerics-disagree-on-crypto-legality-under-islam); [Daily Pakistan, 16 Jul 2026](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/16-Jul-2026/cryptocurrency-is-permissible-says-maulana-bashir-farooq-after-recent-fatwa)
- Reports describe some religious investors in Pakistan, India and the UAE selling holdings after the fatwa — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/world/pakistan-cleric-declares-bitcoin-cryptocurrencies-haram-triggering-investor-sell-off) (weakly sourced). The state crypto regulator (PVARA) engaged Mufti Taqi Usmani directly — [Pakistan Observer](https://pakobserver.net/pakistan-crypto-czar-bilal-bin-saqib-engages-mufti-taqi-usmani-to-make-crypto-halal/)
- UK: in a Muslim Census survey of about 36,000 respondents, about 10% said they had missed university entirely for lack of a non-interest finance option — [Salaam Gateway](https://salaamgateway.com/story/british-muslims-growing-impatient-for-government-to-fulfil-islamic-student-loan-promise) **[older, 2021]**. The UK's Alternative Student Finance is planned from 2026/27 — [University of Leeds](https://www.leeds.ac.uk/muslimstudents) (launch status not confirmed).

**AI as a source of religious answers**
- When GPT-4o was tested on 14 human-issued fatwas about yoga, it was right 6 times and wrong 8, with a bias towards permissiveness — [Review of Islamic Studies, Univ. of Malaya](https://ejournal.um.edu.my/index.php/RIS/article/view/67075)
- Scholarly guidance from July 2026 says ChatGPT may be used for learning, such as terminology or school positions, but not as a mufti — [Hijrah.org](https://hijrah.org/can-muslims-use-chatgpt-fiqh-questions/)
- In a US (general, not Muslim-specific) study, Gen Z ranked faith leaders, then parents, as their most trusted sources on religion, with online search and AI last — [Central Oregon Daily / national wire](https://www.centraloregondaily.com/news/nation-world/why-gen-z-turns-to-parents-over-influencers-for-answers-on-faith/article_cdf3bdf3-fe2d-5480-bcd8-d847ba89ec17.html)

**Mental health**
- ISPU (May 2026, from the 2025 poll): Muslims report worse mental health and more barriers to treatment than other groups — [ISPU](https://ispu.org/?p=36495)
- UK: an Equi report used a December 2025 poll of 1,200 people aged 16–24. Among 253 Muslim respondents, 44% said they might have sought help sooner if services had understood their faith — [Hyphen, Apr 2026](https://hyphenonline.com/2026/04/01/faith-sensitive-mental-health-young-people-muslims-equi/)
- A Muslim Youth Helpline report cites long waiting lists and GPs with poor cultural sensitivity — [The Muslim Vibe](https://themuslimvibe.com/community/new-report-by-muslim-youth-helpline-reveals-the-mental-health-struggle-of-many-young-british-muslims)
- A study of 358 imams in Bangladesh found mental illness widely attributed to sin and spiritual causes — [PMC, Oct 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12528799/)
- Yaqeen's "Our Muslim Youth Are Hurting" (30 interviews, ages 16–20, US) identifies internalised Islamophobia as a key harm — [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/animations/our-muslim-youth-are-hurting-animated-video)

**Palestine and Gaza**
- In 2023–24 a "Quran Book Club" trend on TikTok drew non-Muslim (mostly young, female, American) readers, citing Palestinians' faith. Creator Megan Rice's following grew from 227,400 to 832,100 — [New Lines](https://newlinesmag.com/reportage/on-tiktok-an-unlikely-call-to-islam-emerges). A later piece says the trend thinned out amid backlash and burnout — [Tablet](https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/belief/articles/rise-fall-gaza-converts-islam) **[2023–24]**
- ISPU has published a third report on the Gaza war's effect on religious minorities. Its findings were not retrieved — [WDET](https://wdet.org/?start=3)

### Inferences
- "Will young Pakistanis leave Islam?" is the wrong framing. Exit is rare. The real demand is for *staying with confidence*: answering doubt, hadith scepticism and ethical objections inside a believing frame. Content that treats questioners as enemies is likely to miss this audience. (Inference)
- Pakistani doubters cannot ask openly (blasphemy-law risk, taboo). Anonymous question channels, such as submitted questions, Q&A boxes and WhatsApp forms, are likely more valuable in Pakistan than in the West. (Inference)
- Crypto, stablecoins, freelancing income, bank jobs and riba loans are a hot, time-sensitive "masail" area in 2026. Pakistani scholars are publicly split, so a calm "here are the positions, the evidence and the reasoning" explainer fills a real gap. (Inference)
- The AI-mufti question is new. Content that shows *why* AI answers go wrong, for example by comparing an AI answer with a sourced scholarly answer on screen, is a novel format. (Inference)

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 survey measuring young Pakistanis' religious *doubts* specifically (as opposed to identity or observance) was found.
- Reddit (r/islam, r/pakistan, r/MuslimLounge, r/progressive_islam) and Quora threads were not reachable through the tools. No systematic comment analysis of Urdu Islamic YouTube was found.
- No data was found on marriage anxieties (rishta, delayed marriage, dowry), career and halal-income anxiety (freelancing, bank jobs), or gender questions among Pakistani youth for 2025–26.
- Yaqeen's quantitative doubt paper (Chouhoud, reportedly 600+ US Muslims) was referenced, but its percentages could not be retrieved.
- The ISPU 2025 age-specific religiosity tables and the Gaza-impact report findings could not be retrieved.
- A British Board of Scholars and Imams survey of 205 scholars, reportedly split almost evenly on whether UK student loans are halal, appeared in a search summary, but the source page could not be verified.

## 2. What do audiences complain about in current Islamic content?

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints are an accusatory, rigid or harsh tone; sectarian hostility that viewers copy in comments; unverified and fabricated hadith spreading on social media; celebrity scholars viewers distrust; sermons that ignore real concerns (mental health, career, identity, online ethics); a language and cultural gap in the diaspora; and disrespect towards women. Academic evidence is mostly small and qualitative, but it points the same way.

### Cited Findings
- In a 2025 focus-group study, Pakistani undergraduates aged 20–26 said YouTube Islamic content strengthened some viewers' faith. Others developed exclusionary, rigid interpretations, which participants tied to "lack of empathy, rigidity, and an accusatory tone". Themes included algorithmic exposure and **scepticism toward celebrity scholars and organisations on YouTube** — [Zenodo / JHSS-UoK](https://zenodo.org/records/17297630)
- Comments on Shia/Sunni hate-speech videos on YouTube in Pakistan reuse the speakers' own words ("infidel", "curse", "murderer"). A youth survey found low emotional impact — [Univ. of Canberra thesis](https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/studentTheses/youth-and-hate-speech-the-role-of-religious-leadership-in-the-shi/)
- Among Pakistani Facebook users, religiosity was linked to posting hate speech, while higher education and income were linked to less of it — [JITC, UMT, 2021](https://journals.umt.edu.pk/index.php/JITC/article/view/1333) **[older]**
- A 2025 study from Peshawar reports young people increasingly rely on Facebook, YouTube and TikTok for religious content and have to work their way through sectarian divisions there — [ASSA Journal, 2025](https://assajournal.com/index.php/36/article/download/427/615/658)
- On Arabic social media, fabricated hadith are widely circulated. Debunkers tend to follow academic scholars, while circulators follow Shia accounts, Gulf Sunni public figures and non-professional Sunni pages — [arXiv 2508.07845 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07845v1)
- A Malaysian expert cited a viral "golden umbrella" hadith that clerics in India and Pakistan could not verify — [New Straits Times](https://nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2018/02/338044/be-wary-celebrity-ustaz-spreading-fake-hadith-muslims-told) **[older, 2018]**
- A Nigerian imams' conference heard that sermons fail to address mental health, career paths, identity and online ethics, and that youth feel sidelined from masjid decisions — [The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/news/lemu-conference-tackles-youth-engagement-crisis-reproductive-ethics-in-islam/)
- Indonesia: a UIN Jakarta survey of people aged 17–24 found youth losing interest in mosque-based study and preferring YouTube preachers — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2019/02/10/mosques-losing-sway-over-youth-because-of-online-sermons-study-shows.html) **[older, 2018–19]**
- UK diaspora language gap: sermons "only feel relevant to a motherland that the next generation has never seen" — [H.A. Hellyer](https://www.hahellyer.com/blog/2018/12/why-british-imams-dont-always-speak-the-same-language-as-their-young-followers-national) **[older]**. Local leaders called for imams to preach in both English and Urdu — [Batley News](https://www.batleynews.co.uk/news/fears-that-young-muslims-at-risk-of-being-radicalised-2233011) **[date unclear]**. A Quilliam report says 97% of imams in 512 UK mosques were born overseas — [MEF summary](https://www.meforum.org/islamist-watch/islamic-group-slams-out-of-touch-imams-for) **[older; advocacy source]**
- Disrespect towards women draws backlash. Pakistani youth influencer Sahil Adeem said 95% of Pakistani women are "jahil" on TV, prompting a Sindh Assembly resolution and wide criticism. He was later declared a fugitive in a separate case — [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/sahil-adeem-declared-fugitive-in-indecent-remarks-case); [Bloom Pakistan](https://bloompakistan.com/controversial-tv-show-incident-sahil-adeem-and-khalilur-rehman-criticized-for-misbehaving-with-woman/) **[2024]**
- Women and online authority: research shows Muslim women online cross traditionalist and egalitarian boundaries more readily than offline — [Piela, Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Muslim-Women-Online-Faith-and-Identity-in-Virtual-Space/Piela/p/book/9781138789074) **[older, 2011]**. A Cardiff PhD in progress studies women and the authority of online Islamic content — [Cardiff](https://profiles.cardiff.ac.uk/research-staff/osmanl2)
- The size of demand for women-led learning: Al-Huda (Dr Farhat Hashmi) had an estimated 15,000+ women diploma holders in Pakistan alone — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna37959628) **[older, ~2010]**. Its spread was aided by online classes in English and Urdu streamed from Canada — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Huda_International)
- Wazaif and remedies content draws large audiences: "Quranic Wazaif by Shahzad Mirza" has about 605K subscribers (June 2025) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC8bFoIpk0mDVgCp1-F2mw9A) (third-party estimate)

### Inferences
- The strongest *differentiators* available to a new creator are the opposites of these complaints: a calm tone, no attacks on other maslaks, every hadith shown with its grading and source on screen, and topics drawn from viewers' real lives. (Inference, supported by the Zenodo and Canberra findings)
- "Celebrity scholar" fatigue suggests that transparency, such as showing the book page, explaining *how* a ruling was reached, or saying "I don't know / scholars differ", could build trust better than charisma. (Inference)
- Women are a large, under-addressed audience in Urdu online content. Material on women's fiqh, marriage rights and mental load, delivered respectfully, is a gap. (Inference; direct survey evidence not found)

### Gaps
- No systematic Urdu YouTube comment analysis or survey on complaints such as clickbait thumbnails, monotony or lack of depth was found.
- No 2025–26 survey of women's unmet needs in Islamic content was found.
- A Sarwar et al. (2020) survey of 543 people in Pakistan's provincial capitals reportedly found 6.6% take part in sect-related online discussions, but the source page was not verifiable.

## 3. Which comparative-religion, interfaith and da'wah formats work, and are they under-served in Urdu/Hindi?

### Takeaway
English-language comparative religion has a long, polemical track record (Zakir Naik's style). Urdu/Hindi has very recently shown huge demand for *serious, civil, debate-style* engagement with atheism: the December 2025 Mufti Shamail Nadwi vs Javed Akhtar debate drew over 10M views. Respectful Hindu-Muslim and Christian-Muslim dialogue content in Urdu/Hindi appears thin. The existing Urdu atheism material is mostly courses, not mass-audience video.

### Cited Findings
- The "Does God Exist?" debate between Mufti Shamail Nadwi (b. 1998, founder of Markaz al-Wahyain / Wahyain Foundation) and Javed Akhtar took place on 20 Dec 2025 at the Constitution Club, New Delhi, moderated by Saurabh Dwivedi (Lallantop). Nadwi argued from contingency; Akhtar pressed the problem of evil and free will — [Wikipedia: Shamail Nadwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamail_Nadwi)
- A Quint commentary says the debate passed 10 million views across the Lallantop and Nadwi YouTube channels — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/opinion/javed-akhtar-mufti-shamail-nadwi-debate-does-god-exist-muslim-liberal-atheism-islam). An Ahmadi outlet analysed "what the debate left unanswered" — [Al Hakam](https://www.alhakam.org/?p=82266)
- The debate's origin was contentious: Wahyain objected to a Bengal Urdu Academy event featuring Akhtar, the academy postponed it, and rights groups criticised the postponement — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1086129/rights-groups-criticise-bengal-urdu-academy-for-postponing-event-to-be-attended-by-javed-akhtar). A later viral clip of Nadwi saying Muslims should put Shariah above nation and courts drew criticism — [NewsGram](https://www.newsgram.com/religion/2025/12/29/mufti-shamail-nadwi-instructs-indian-muslims-to-follow-islamic-law)
- Zakir Naik's 2024 tour of Pakistan (government-invited, from 30 Sep 2024, with lectures in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad and a "Dr. Zakir Se Poochiye" Q&A session) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2024/09/30/dr-zakir-naik-arrives-in-pakistan-for-15-day-tour/amp/). Minority leaders objected to his remarks about other faiths — [UCA News](https://www.ucanews.com/news/pakistani-minorities-deplore-preachers-derogatory-remarks/106730). One Pakistani commentary called the visit a "flop" — [The Friday Times](https://thefridaytimes.com/07-Oct-2024/dr-zakir-naik-s-visit-to-pakistan-a-flop-show) **[2024]**
- Naik's core audience of urban, young Muslims reportedly prefers English to Urdu sermons — [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/columns/comment/zakir-naik-a-different-kind-of-jihadist) **[older]**
- Urdu atheism material exists mainly as courses: a two-year Urdu certificate course on atheism — [eMahad](https://campus.emahad.org/course/view.php?id=429); Al-Balagh Academy's "Atheism and Islam – Level 1" offered in Urdu translation for the first time — [Al-Balagh](https://www.albalaghacademy.org/)
- Respectful Hindu-Muslim formats: a BBC film paired a Muslim influencer and a Hindu vlogger discussing faith and social media — [BBC](https://bbc.in/2VbwxHG). The CPS (Maulana Wahiduddin Khan tradition) publishes on "successful Hindu-Muslim dialogue" — [CPS Global](https://cpsglobal.org/books/shared-wisdom/successful-hindu-muslim-dialogue). Böll Foundation publication "Muslim-Hindu Dialogue: Issues-Queries-Arguments" — [Böll India](https://in.boell.org/en/2013/12/10/muslim-hindu-dialogue-issues-queries-arguments-publications) **[older, 2013]**
- An academic article argues that interfaith relations on YouTube are neither new nor revolutionary, and that lax moderation has let creators vent contempt for other religions — [Sydney open journal](https://openjournals.library.sydney.edu.au/LA/article/view/5017/5715)
- A qualitative study of Christian, Hindu and Muslim Gen Y and Gen Z in Mumbai examines digital religious influence — [Religions 16(1), 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel16010073)
- Pakistani Gen Z with higher religious tolerance shows more interfaith harmony, and education and social-media exposure strengthen that link — [The Knowledge journal](https://theknowledge.com.pk/index.php/tn/article/download/123/154)
- Cross-border risk: after the Pahalgam attack (April 2025), India blocked 16 Pakistani YouTube channels for "inciting communal hatred" — [Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/news/news-amp.php?id=1524766)

### Inferences
- The Nadwi–Akhtar numbers show that an Urdu/Hindi audience will watch long, argument-heavy content on God's existence when it is staged as a civil, moderated event. Pakistan has no visible equivalent mass-audience format yet. (Inference)
- The polemical Naik style now carries reputational costs: minority objections, bans and mixed reception. A "respectful dialogue" format (guest from another faith, shared questions, no point-scoring) is open in Urdu. It would also be safer for reach in India, where Pakistani channels can be geo-blocked. (Inference / speculation)
- Turning existing course-style Urdu atheism material into short reels and explainers is an obvious open space. (Inference)

### Gaps
- No subscriber or view data was found for any Urdu/Hindi channel doing respectful Hindu-Muslim or Christian-Muslim dialogue. No such channel could be confirmed as existing at scale.
- No evidence was found on Christian-Muslim dialogue content in Urdu within Pakistan.
- The 10M-view figure comes from one opinion piece. No neutral analytics were found.

## 4. Which innovative formats worldwide are worth adapting, and what engagement evidence exists?

### Takeaway
Proven English-language models include serialised story series (Yaqeen's *The Firsts*), daily Ramadan Qur'an series with rotating guests (*Qur'an 30 for 30*), deep-interview podcasts with academics funded by members (*The Thinking Muslim*), scholar-checked hadith-authenticity Q&A (*HadithAnswers*), communal "reading the Qur'an together" (TikTok's Quran Book Club), and high-production children's animation (*Omar & Hana*). Hard engagement numbers are scarce. The Thinking Muslim and Omar & Hana are the best documented.

### Cited Findings
- **Serialised scholar-led history:** *The Firsts* (Yaqeen, Omar Suleiman) is a weekly video series on the lives of the Sahaba — [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/blog/introducing-new-yaqeen-series-the-firsts-the-forerunners-of-islam). View counts were not found.
- **Daily Ramadan micro-curriculum:** *Qur'an 30 for 30* covers one juz' per day, with a different scholar each day, published as both video and podcast with transcripts. It ran for at least 3 seasons — [Yaqeen](https://www.yaqeeninstitute.org/listen/quran-30-for-30); [Season 3](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/blog/quran-30-for-30-season-3)
- **Scholar plus academic podcast, community-funded:** *The Thinking Muslim* (Muhammad Jalal) has about 150K subscribers roughly 14 months after launch (self-reported) — [Substack](https://jalalayn.substack.com/p/help-us-take-the-thinking-muslim). vidIQ showed about 285K in June 2025, at 2 uploads a week — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCWJPKXhkcMGXafdtqGx1mEw). Its Substack newsletter has 12K+ subscribers — [Substack](https://jalalayn.substack.com/). It runs on memberships and donations, and Gaza coverage was central.
- **Hadith verification as a service:** *HadithAnswers* answers user-submitted questions on whether a narration is authentic, checked by named hadith scholars and citing classical critics (Ibn al-Jawzi, Dhahabi, Ibn Hajar). It has flagged popular fabricated narrations — [HadithAnswers](https://hadithanswers.com/fabricated-hadith-with-a-trustworthy-chain-of-narrators/)
- **Academic backing for "fact-check" content:** debunkers of fabricated hadith on social media cluster around academic scholars — [arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07845v1). ## 5. Which niche positions are uncrowded in Urdu?

### Takeaway
Two large Urdu niches are visibly occupied but contested. The "non-sectarian, book-referenced" critic is Engineer Muhammad Ali Mirza (~3.2M subscribers), now facing a 295-C blasphemy case. The "rational, modernist" scholar is Javed Ahmad Ghamidi, whom some youth research links with anti-hadith attitudes. Sect-specific giants (Tariq Jameel, Dawat-e-Islami) dominate general reach. No source showed a calm, traditionally trained *and* academically literate, bilingual, openly referenced creator focused on hadith verification, modern questions or Islamic intellectual history in Urdu. This looks like the open niche, but the conclusion rests on what the searches did *not* find.

### Cited Findings
- Engineer Muhammad Ali Mirza is listed as "non-denominational" with about 3.19M subscribers — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Mirza); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC5VYosjbcRb3yKfvl-YuJFw). His logic-driven, step-by-step style attracted educated youth, and his questioning of common beliefs creates both curiosity and controversy — [The Pakistan Connect](https://thepakistanconnect.com/engineer-ali-mirza-support-and-criticism-in-pakistan/)
- He was detained on 26 Aug 2025 in Jhelum under MPO and then booked under PPC 295-C and PECA. The CII called his remarks blasphemous and urged strict action — [Nukta](https://nukta.com/cii-mirza-muhammad-ali-engineer); [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/blasphemy-case-against-engineer-mirza-highlights-pakistans-contentious-laws). The Islamabad High Court later (May 2026) declared the CII opinion null and void — [Digital Rights Foundation](https://digitalrightsfoundation.pk/ihc-rules-cii-opinion-on-blasphemy-case-unlawful-reinforces-judicial-authority/). The Lahore High Court granted bail — [Pakistan Observer](https://pakobserver.net/relief-for-engineer-mirza-as-lhc-overturns-session-courts-bail-rejection/). His current trial status was not found.
- The Punjab youth study associates the modern anti-hadith movement with Ghulam Ahmad Parvez and Javed Ahmad Ghamidi, and finds youth attitudes toward such movements "moderately high" — [UM thesis](https://cendekia.unisza.edu.my/neuaxis-e/Record/um-14412). Ghamidi's lectures and Qur'an translation are distributed through GCIL podcast and YouTube channels — [GCIL Podbean](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/page/53/) (audience size not found)
- Sect-specific reach: Dawat-e-Islami has about 1.03M subscribers (April 2025) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC7xiEjMTy-8EvujnY4K6Mvw). Tariq Jamil is a Deobandi preacher with a very large audience — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_Jamil)

- In India, a young, madrasa-trained mufti who argues philosophically (Nadwi, b. 1998) reached a mass audience on the God question — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamail_Nadwi). This shows demand for a traditionally credentialed figure who is also intellectually fluent.
- Urdu academic writing on youth secularism and hadith rejection exists in Pakistani journals ([Al-Irfan](https://ojs.mul.edu.pk/index.php/alirfan/article/view/1058), [Al-Amir](https://alamir.com.pk/index.php/ojs/article/view/232)), which suggests the material is available but not yet turned into popular media.

### Inferences (niche candidates; speculative where stated)
- **"Muhaqqiq, not munazir":** a calm, research-backed and non-polemical scholar who shows sources on screen. EMAM proves the demand for "show me the book" content, while his legal troubles show that *attacking* other groups or touching sensitive persons is dangerous in Pakistan. A creator who keeps the referencing but drops the confrontation, and explicitly respects classical scholarship, would hold a defensible position. (Inference)
- **Hadith verification in Urdu:** given the Quranism and hadith-scepticism signals (Q1) and the fabricated-hadith problem (Q2), a series that explains *why* a hadith is sahih or da'if (isnad, rijal, the grading by named muhaddithin) answers sceptics and corrects viral fakes at once. No Urdu equivalent of HadithAnswers was found. (Inference)
- **The Dars-e-Nizami plus university bridge, bilingual Urdu/English:** diaspora youth face a language gap (Q2), and Pakistan's English-medium youth also consume English content (Naik's audience preference). Urdu content with English subtitles and English key terms, or the reverse, can serve both. (Inference)
- **Islamic intellectual history in Urdu** (the muhaddithin, Ghazali, Ibn Khaldun, Shah Waliullah, the Indian ulama tradition) as stories: no competitor was found in searches. (Speculation; needs channel-level verification)
- **Crypto and modern-finance masail explainers** that set out the differing positions with evidence: the June–July 2026 fatwa dispute created immediate demand. (Inference)
- **Risk note:** the blasphemy-law environment means content on sects, the Companions, hadith rejection or atheism needs careful wording. Legal and sensitivity review should be part of the production process. (Inference based on the EMAM case and the EUAA report)
