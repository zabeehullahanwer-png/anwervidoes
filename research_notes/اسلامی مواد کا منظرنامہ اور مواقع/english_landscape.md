# English-Language Islamic Scholars, Institutes and Da'wah Creators: A Global Map (2025–2026)

> **Method note for the report writer.** Research date: 22 Oct 2026. WebFetch could not reach most primary sites from this environment (Wikipedia, yaqeeninstitute.org and salaamgateway.com all returned DNS errors). Most findings below therefore come from search-engine snippets of those pages and from third-party stats trackers (vidIQ, Social Blade, SocialCounts, SpeakRJ, CreatorDB, SponsorRadar, Playboard). These trackers **estimate** subscriber counts, and their snapshot dates vary. Every count below carries the date the source gave. Where no date was given, it is marked "undated". Counts are YouTube subscribers unless another platform is named.

## 1. Who are the leading voices and organisations, and how big are they?

### Takeaway
There are four tiers of English-language Islamic YouTube. (1) Mass-reach "reminder" brands at 4–6M+: Mufti Menk, MercifulServant, Zakir Naik. (2) Institutional and educator brands at about 2–3M: Bayyinah/Nouman Ali Khan, Yaqeen Institute, OnePath Network. (3) Debate, street-da'wah and apologetics personalities at about 0.5–1.4M: Mohammed Hijab, Ali Dawah, Assim al-Hakeem, Muslim Lantern, Blogging Theology, One Message Foundation. (4) Scholar-academics and colleges whose influence is larger than their channel numbers: Yasir Qadhi (~755K), Omar Suleiman's personal channel (~535K, but ~3.3M on Instagram), Hamza Yusuf/Zaytuna, Abdal Hakim Murad/Cambridge Muslim College, Jonathan Brown.

### Inferences
- **Reach and scholarly depth are inversely related on YouTube.** Short reminder and recitation channels (MercifulServant ~4.7M) and short-clip preachers (Menk ~6.3M) outscale research institutes (Yaqeen ~2.2M) and academic scholars (Qadhi ~755K). Qadhi has far fewer YouTube subscribers than Menk, yet he is among the most cited US scholars.
- **Personal brands spread across platforms.** Omar Suleiman has about 6x more Instagram followers (~3.3M) than personal-YouTube subscribers (~535K), and his long-form work lives on Yaqeen's channel. This suggests he uses Instagram for short reminders and the institution's channel for series.
- **Growth has slowed for legacy channels.** Bayyinah's 30-day growth is flat and Omar Suleiman's personal channel gains about 200/day. Ali Dawah (+~65K from Apr 2025 to Jul 2026) and Muslim Lantern (+~6K/month) are still growing. This points to debate and short formats gaining ground in 2025–26.
- **Geographic hubs:** the UK dominates debate and street da'wah (Hyde Park Speakers' Corner). Texas (Dallas/Irving/Plano) is the hub of US institutional da'wah (Yaqeen, Bayyinah, EPIC/Qadhi). Sydney (OnePath) and Melbourne (Assaad) anchor Australia. Malaysia hosts Zakir Naik. Jeddah hosts English Q&A (Assim al-Hakeem). Southern Africa produces Menk.

- **Muslim 500 2026 placements** for Menk, Yusuf, Murad, Qadhi, Suleiman and Brown were not retrieved. The full list is at themuslim500.com.
- **Coverage of the Gulf beyond Jeddah, South Africa (e.g., Radio Islam), Canada and English-language Malaysia (beyond Naik)** was not researched in depth.
- **TikTok and Instagram figures** for most figures are missing or come only from low-quality aggregators.

## 2. What formats are used?

### Takeaway
The successful brands combine several formats. Yaqeen pairs peer-reviewed papers with animations, video essays, infographics, podcasts and Ramadan series. Bayyinah pairs free YouTube clips with a paid tafsir and Arabic platform and in-person intensives. Debaters use livestreams, Speakers' Corner clips and long debates. Mass-reach channels rely on short reminder clips and recitation videos. Audio is redistributed through aggregator networks such as Muslim Central.

### Cited Findings
- **Yaqeen's stated formats:** "peer-reviewed papers, articles, videos, infographics, and podcast episodes". The videos are described as "scholarly video essays, lectures, and animated explainers", and the infographics as "visual research summaries" — [Yaqeen llms.txt](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/llms.txt).
- **Yaqeen papers:** 244 papers are listed on its papers page — [Yaqeen papers](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/papers.md). Yaqeen FAQ material surfaced in search says "over 260 peer-reviewed papers and research reports", all "free to read, free to share, and free to cite" — [Yaqeen llms.txt](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/llms.txt). The 244 vs 260 difference is likely a matter of snapshot date.
- **Yaqeen academic journal:** *Hashiya* is a forthcoming online and print journal for specialist audiences — [Yaqeen call for papers](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/call-for-papers); [Yaqeen Journals](https://journals.yaqeeninstitute.org/).
- **Yaqeen animations:** described as the way "research publications come to life, providing quick, digestible explanations in an innovative, visual style". Released free — [Yaqeen blog, 2019](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/blog/top-5-animations-of-2019-countdown-to-2020).
- **Bayyinah:** a paid streaming platform (Bayyinah TV), formerly a ten-month on-campus "Dream" classical Arabic program in Dallas/Fort Worth (2010–11 era) — [MuslimMatters 2010](https://muslimmatters.org/2010/04/14/bayyinah-institute-first-full-time-classical-arabic-on-campus-program-in-the-us/). - **Interview podcasts:** The Thinking Muslim's guest conversations, plus a paid membership and a newsletter — [Substack](https://jalalayn.substack.com/); [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/se/podcast/the-thinking-muslim/id1471798762). Blogging Theology runs scholar interviews and translates them into Arabic, French, German and Indonesian — [Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/Bloggingtheology).

### Inferences
- **The "research paper → animation → infographic → podcast" funnel is Yaqeen's distinctive format stack.** It turns one research asset into several media assets, and its peers seem not to copy it at the same scale.
- **Short-form is the entry point for every tier.** The ladder runs from short clips to long-form series to paid courses, academies or degrees (Bayyinah TV, CMC Teachable, Sapience Academy, Zaytuna).
- **Debate brands rely on live and unscripted formats** (Speakers' Corner, livestreams, reaction videos). These are cheap to produce and generate conflict-driven engagement.

### Gaps
- No primary data was found on Yaqeen's app or Yaqeen Academy, or on enrolment and course numbers at Bayyinah TV, Sapience Academy or CMC (beyond the price examples).
- Mufti Menk's short-clip format (length, vertical vs horizontal) is not documented in any source retrieved.
- No source was found on Islam21c's formats (articles vs video) or Digital Mimbar's formats.

## 3. Production and editing styles

### Takeaway
Primary documentation of editing styles is thin. The evidence that exists shows three things. Institutes invest in animation and polished video essays (Yaqeen, OnePath). Debate and street channels use raw handheld or livestream footage with reaction edits. A commercial market for "Muslim-only" editors produces explainer channels with icon-based visuals and recurring avatars. Academic studies of da'wah YouTube (mostly Indonesian) list motion graphics, animated visualisation and on-screen text. They also note that some channels add religious background music, so "no music" is a choice made by some channels, not a universal norm.

### Cited Findings
- **Yaqeen animations:** described as "quick, digestible explanations in an innovative, visual style" built from its research papers — [Yaqeen 2019](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/blog/top-5-animations-of-2019-countdown-to-2020). Yaqeen's video types are "scholarly video essays, lectures, and animated explainers" — [Yaqeen llms.txt](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/llms.txt).
- **Yaqeen Ramadan series use trailers.** For example, "Trailer: Jannah: Home at Last | Ramadan Series" — [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/jannah-home-at-last/trailer-jannah-home-at-last-ramadan-series.md). "The Name I Need" (Ramadan 2026) also had an official trailer — [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/series/the-name-i-need-ramadan-2026.md).
- **Smile2Jannah** combines animation and special effects with a light-hearted tone on contemporary topics — [Muslim 500](https://themuslim500.com/?p=56237).
- **MercifulServant** says donations fund "video hosting, production, and subtitles", which indicates that subtitling is a core production cost — [GoFundMe](https://www.gofundme.com/f/mercifulservantfund).
- **Blogging Theology's Patreon** funds "production costs including a producer" and "professional translations" into Arabic, French, German and Indonesian — [Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/Bloggingtheology).
- **Commercial explainer style:** a current Upwork job for an Islamic explainer channel specifies "a recurring teacher avatar/character, icon-based visuals", pacing with "literal visual metaphors", and requires the editor to be a practising Muslim — [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Muslim-Video-Editor-Needed-for-Islamic-Explainer-Channel_~022065597927262553375).
- **Academic evidence (Indonesia):** da'wah YouTubers add "animated visualization", "unique descriptions for important material points" and "religious music back sounds" — [Garuda/Indonesian study](https://garuda.kemdikbud.go.id/documents/detail/2053984). A 2023 study of the ANB Channel credits live streams, motion graphics and video podcasts as its strategies. It criticises "excessively long video durations" and "limited variation in style" — [UII ICONISH 2023](https://fpscs.uii.ac.id/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ICONISH-Article-19_157-170-Analysis-of-Creative-Videography-Strategies_Anggi-Arif-Fudin-Setiadi.pdf).
- **Speakers' Corner as content set:** "social media has fundamentally changed Speakers' Corner… some of the more frequent speakers at the corner have become notorious online" — [MEL Magazine](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/how-a-historic-corner-of-london-became-a-real-life-4chan); see also [The Spectator](https://spectator.com/article/what-happened-to-speakers-corner/).
- **Early capacity-building:** a 2008 MuslimMatters project aimed to train 200 Muslims to "produce, edit, market and distribute" da'wah videos — [MuslimMatters 2008](https://muslimmatters.org/2008/03/12/producing-online-video/).
- **Short-video case (non-Sunni, UK):** a London "young imam" built an audience "one short video at a time" — [Fetzer Institute case study](https://fetzer.org/case-study/londons-young-imam-is-changing-how-people-see-islam-one-short-video-at-a-time/).

### Inferences
- **Two distinct visual grammars exist.**
  - (a) A produced, studio or cinematic grammar: animation, trailers, a consistent series branding and polished thumbnails. Examples are Yaqeen, OnePath and Bayyinah.
  - (b) A raw, documentary or live grammar: handheld street footage, livestream captures, and reaction or split-screen edits, with confrontational thumbnails. Examples are Speakers' Corner channels, Muslim Lantern, Ali Dawah and DawahWise.
- **Subtitles and translation are treated as a growth lever.** Both MercifulServant and Blogging Theology name them as funded costs, which means multilingual captioning is a recognised driver of reach.
- **Many English-language Islamic channels avoid music**, using voice, Quran recitation or ambient sound, but the evidence is incomplete. The Indonesian evidence shows the norm varies, so the report should present no-music audio as a positioning choice, not an industry standard.

### Gaps
- No interviews or articles with production heads were found (Yaqeen creative team, OnePath studio, Bayyinah media team, Mufti Menk's media team). There is no documented data on studio sets, camera setups, thumbnail A/B testing, hook structure, caption styles or music policies for the named channels.
- No source confirmed OnePath's "cinematic" style beyond the cinema screening of "Last Chance".
- MercifulServant's founder and its exact audio policy (recitation only vs nasheeds) were not confirmed.

## 4. Series structures and release cadence

### Takeaway
Ramadan is the main release window. Yaqeen and Omar Suleiman release a nightly series of 30–31 episodes almost every year (2021 "Meeting Muhammad"; 2023 "Jannah: Home at Last"). Alongside these run parallel Ramadan formats such as "Qur'an 30 for 30" (one juz per night, now in at least its 5th season), Taraweeh-break reflections and multiple concurrent 2026 series. Long-running serialised lecture courses, such as Yasir Qadhi's Seerah of roughly 100 episodes, are the evergreen counterpart. Debate creators publish continuously or weekly rather than in series.

### Cited Findings
- **"Meeting Muhammad" (Ramadan 2021):** 31 episodes, all presented by Dr Omar Suleiman — [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/series/meeting-muhammad-a-ramadan-series.md).
- **"Jannah: Home at Last" (Ramadan 2023):** 31 episodes. Ep. 1 ("Jannah Is Waiting for You") was published 22 Mar 2023 and Ep. 7 ("The Fragrance and Weather of Jannah") 27 Mar 2023 — [Yaqeen series](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/series/jannah-home-at-last.md); [Ep. 1](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/jannah-home-at-last/jannah-is-waiting-for-you-episode-1.md). Ep. 26, "Jannat al-Firdaus", was published 16 Apr 2023 — [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/jannah-home-at-last/jannat-al-firdaus-episode-26.md). **Note:** the "Firdaus" series the brief mentions appears to be this 2023 Jannah series, which has a Firdaus episode, rather than a series with that title.
- **"Qur'an 30 for 30":** a Yaqeen Ramadan series in which each episode covers one juz'. It is hosted by Dr Omar Suleiman and Sh. Abdullah Oduro with a different guest scholar each night (e.g., Season 3: Dr Ovamir Anjum, Dr Rania Awaad, Ust. Faatimah Knight, Sh. Yaser Birjas and Dr Zohair Abdul-Rahman) — [Yaqeen S3](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/blog/quran-30-for-30-season-3); [Juz 26 page](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/juz-26-with-dr-ovamir-anjum-quran-30-for-30-season-3). - **Taraweeh Reflections (2024)** — [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/taraweeh-reflections-2024/3-ways-the-sahabah-would-start-ramadan-taraweeh-reflections-dr-omar-suleiman.md). **Ramadan Reflections (2025):** 24 short reflections given in Taraweeh breaks by Suleiman, Imam Tom and Sh. Episode titles include "Seerah Part 87 – Battle of Tabuk (1)" — [Chartable](https://chartable.com/charts/itunes/ie-islam-episodes).
- **Qalam Institute (Abdul Nasir Jangda, Texas):** the "Ramadan Tafseer Series" ran around 2010–2012 on MuslimMatters (Surah Yaseen and other topics) — [MuslimMatters 2011](https://muslimmatters.org/2011/08/01/the-ramadan-tafseer-series-surah-yaseen-part-1-abdulnasir-jangda/); [MuslimMatters 2010](https://muslimmatters.org/2010/09/07/the-ramadan-tafseer-series-concluding-remarks-l-abdulnasir-jangda/). ### Inferences
- **The "one episode per Ramadan night" format, with about 30 episodes, a trailer and a single theme (Prophet, Jannah, Names of Allah), is the flagship pattern for institutional brands.** By 2025–26, Yaqeen ran several Ramadan products at once: a nightly theme series, short Taraweeh reflections and Qur'an 30 for 30.
- **Ensemble formats (one host plus a rotating guest scholar)** spread production load and cross-promote scholars within one institution's network.
- **There are two volume strategies.** Q&A and clip volume (Assim al-Hakeem, ~13.9K videos; Menk, ~5.8K) contrasts with institutional seasonal series (Yaqeen, ~3.5K videos across many speakers).

### Gaps
- Omar Suleiman's Ramadan series between 2017 and 2020, and for 2022, 2024 and 2025, were not enumerated (e.g., his "Angels in Your Presence" book suggests an earlier series, but this is unverified).
- The start and end dates and the venue of the Qadhi Seerah series were not confirmed by a retrieved source. The commonly cited 2011–2017 timeframe at a Memphis mosque is **unverified**.
- No Mufti Menk, Bayyinah or Nouman Ali Khan Ramadan series titles or cadences were retrieved.
- No source showed Ramadan vs non-Ramadan view spikes.

## 5. Business and funding models, and team size

### Takeaway
There are four main models. **Donor-funded non-profit institutes** (Yaqeen: about $8.76M revenue in 2023). **Subscription education** (Bayyinah TV; CMC Teachable courses; Zaytuna tuition). **Creator crowdfunding and memberships** (Patreon, own membership pages, GoFundMe, OnePath's "dollar-a-day"). **Speaking and touring** for star preachers. Team sizes are almost entirely undisclosed.

## 6. What distinguishes "scholar-researcher" brands from viral preachers?

### Takeaway
Scholar-researcher brands build credibility through **institutional and academic markers**: peer review, editorial boards, citations, university posts, accreditation and free citable papers. They accept slower growth and lower subscriber counts in return. Viral preachers and debaters build **personal charisma, conflict and relatability** on platform-native formats, and grow faster. Academic and journalistic literature frames them as "alternative" or democratised authority, and some critics link a segment to a "Muslim manosphere".

### Cited Findings
- **Yaqeen peer review:** papers undergo internal review by its research team and external scholarly review before publication. Its editorial board and peer review committee include "scholars who represent diverse institutions of traditional Islamic learning as well as intellectuals from various fields". Articles "may also be updated or archived" — [Yaqeen: Scholarly Rigor](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/about-us/our-commitment-to-scholarly-rigor); [Editorial Board](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/yaqeen-institute/meet-the-editorial-board-and-peer-review-committee). Its stated research goals include refuting claims made against Islam — [Yaqeen: submit proposal](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/submit-your-paper-proposal).
- **University credentials as a brand asset:** Brown holds a Georgetown chair and edits an Oxford encyclopedia — [Yaqeen](https://cdn.yaqeeninstitute.org/?p=178). Murad holds a Cambridge lectureship — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Winter). Zaytuna has WSCUC accreditation — [WSCUC](https://www.wscuc.org/institutions/zaytuna-college/?print=pdf).
- **Gary R. Bunt, *Islamic Algorithms: Online Influence in the Muslim Metaverse* (Bloomsbury, hardback 2024; paperback Nov 2025):** uses "algorithm" as a metaphor for influences and influencers shaping Islamic understanding. Argues that apps and social networks now inform new generations. Has 11 chapters, including Sunni/Shi'i online authority and future "Muslim metaverses" — [UWTSD](https://WWW.UWTSD.AC.UK/news/new-book-alert-islamic-algorithms-online-influence-muslim-metaverse-professor-gary-r-bunt); [Frankfurt review PDF](https://www.fb09.uni-frankfurt.de/167712037.pdf). It follows his *Hashtag Islam* (2018) — [same review](https://www.fb09.uni-frankfurt.de/167712037.pdf).
- **YouTube's openness lets preachers gain authority "because of the democratic nature that social media provides"** (study of Indonesian preachers) — [Hamdani 2023, Hikmatuna](https://e-journal.uingusdur.ac.id/hikmatuna/article/view/pps915).
- **A Bourdieu-based study (2022–25 videos):** one preacher builds authority through emotional narratives of spiritual healing, another through intellectual and socio-political discussion. Digital media "reconfigured" rather than replaced traditional authority — [Muharrik journal](https://ejournal.insuriponorogo.ac.id/index.php/muharrik/article/view/9658/6002).
- **Malaysia:** an ISEAS paper argues that online tools have made the preaching licence (tauliah) less relevant and preachers harder to regulate — [MalaysiaNow, Dec 2020](https://malaysianow.com/news/2020/12/23/religious-authorities-losing-grip-on-islamic-narrative-as-preachers-go-online). One Malaysian preacher has about 743K TikTok followers and uses the platform to reach youth — [Free Malaysia Today, May 2021](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2021/05/18/talking-religion-on-tiktok).
- **"Muslim manosphere" framing:** commentary names Haqiqatjou, "Dawah Man", Ali Dawah and Mohammed Hijab as a cluster. It argues this da'wah style is "far less about inviting people to Islam" and more about asserting a conservative strand — [Hyphen, May 2023](https://hyphenonline.com/2023/05/02/humza-yousaf-influencers-have-no-right-to-declare-anyone-an-apostate/); [MWN Hub](https://mwnhub.com/read-detail.php?id=294). An AAR session abstract says prominent Muslim YouTube influencers "frame red pill ideology as 'traditional' Muslim masculinity" — [AAR](https://papers.aarweb.org/session/social-media-manosphere-and-making-masculinities-online). A BBC segment reports young Muslims saying these stars explain the faith "in a more relatable way than the imams… in the mosque" — [BBC via Univ. of Auckland archive](https://collections.library.auckland.ac.nz/tv-radio/title/TR_13332_04). Wikipedia describes Haqiqatjou as associated with the "Muslim manosphere" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Haqiqatjou).
- **Controversy as a feature of debate brands:** the Community Security Trust and a local synagogue reported Hijab's 2021 Golders Green remarks to police — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Hijab). Critical advocacy coverage links him to the 2022 Leicester unrest; this is a hostile source — [Middle East Forum](https://www.meforum.org/fwi/fwi-news-islamist-provocateur-to-speak-near-toronoto). The Muslim Skeptic is rated "questionable" — [MBFC](https://mediabiasfactcheck.com/muslim-skeptic-bias/). Abu Layth has faced criticism from other scholars and a fabricated-clip incident — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Layth); [Lancashire Telegraph](https://www.thisislancashire.co.uk/news/19308410.thugs-record-breaking-house-muslim-youtuber-fake-video/).
- **Recognition systems:** the Muslim 500 (Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre, Jordan) lists both scholarly figures (e.g., Omar Suleiman; Menk since 2010) and da'wah media figures (e.g., Mohammed Hijab; Zeeshan Ali/Smile2Jannah) — [RNS bio](https://religionnews.com/author/cap-omar-suleiman/); [muftimenk.com](https://muftimenk.com/home-elementor/); [Muslim 500 Hijab](https://themuslim500.com/profiles/hijab-mohammed/); [Muslim 500 Zeeshan Ali](https://themuslim500.com/?p=56237).

### Inferences
- **The distinctive "scholar-researcher" signals are:**
  1. Named credentials, such as a PhD or a university chair.
  2. Institutional review processes, such as peer review and an editorial board.
  3. Free, citable long-form texts behind the videos.
  4. Multi-scholar ensembles instead of a single personality.
  5. Calm, studio-produced, series-based output.
  6. Accredited or structured education pathways.
- **The distinctive viral-preacher signals are:**
  1. A single charismatic host.
  2. Conflict and debate hooks.
  3. Live or unscripted footage.
  4. High posting volume.
  5. Reaction formats.
  6. Positioning against perceived liberal or secular opponents. In some cases this means gendered "alpha" framing.
- **The most successful brands combine elements of both.** Omar Suleiman pairs Instagram-scale reach with Yaqeen's research backing. Mufti Menk pairs short-reminder virality with a decades-long traditional scholarly profile. Mohammed Hijab pairs street debate with an institute (Sapience) and formal university debates.
- **The academic literature retrieved is mostly about Indonesia and Malaysia.** English-language Western "YouTube imam" studies did not come up in search, which points to a relative research gap.

### Gaps
- No Guardian, NYT, New Lines, Middle East Eye or Al Jazeera long-form pieces on English-language da'wah YouTubers were retrieved, despite targeted searches. The BBC "Muslim manosphere" segment's date and title were not confirmed.
- No peer-reviewed study specifically on Yaqeen, Bayyinah or UK Speakers' Corner da'wah channels was found. Gary Bunt's book is the main scholarly reference, but its chapter-level treatment of specific English-language influencers was not verified.
- No audience-demographic data (age, gender, country split) was found for any channel.
