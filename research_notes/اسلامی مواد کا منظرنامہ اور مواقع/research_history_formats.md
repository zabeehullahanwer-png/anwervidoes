# Research-driven, historical and intellectual Islamic content online (2025–2026): formats that work, and Urdu gaps

*Method note for the report writer: research done 22 Oct 2026. WebFetch and curl could not reach most hosts from this environment (DNS failures or proxy 403s), so the figures below come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from full-text reads. Subscriber and view counts are snapshots, often from third-party trackers, and are dated where the source gives a date. Treat them as approximate.*

## 1. Which channels and projects make Islamic history and civilisation engaging, and why do their formats work?

### Takeaway
The formats that work are (a) animated, map-led narrative history (Kings and Generals, Al Muqaddimah, Hikma History), (b) prestige documentary and exhibition brands (Al Jazeera Documentary, 1001 Inventions), (c) dramatised history whose audience then looks for more history (Ertugrul in Urdu), and (d) recurring biography series (Yaqeen's *The Firsts*). They have three things in common: one story per episode, strong visuals (maps, animation, reconstructions) and a regular release schedule. On short-form platforms, "edit"-style history clips get the most likes. Their sourcing is usually thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Kings and Generals** (animated military-history channel) has done multi-episode arcs on early Islamic history: Khalid ibn al-Walid's campaigns in Syria and Iraq, the conquest of Egypt, the First Fitna, Siffin, the Second Fitna, Karbala and Marj Rahit, and the Abbasid Revolution. — [TheTVDB ep. list](https://www.thetvdb.com/series/kings-and-generals/episodes/9332474); [TheTVDB 2021 ep.](https://thetvdb.com/series/kings-and-generals/episodes/8379938); [World History Encyclopedia: Early Muslim Expansion](https://www.worldhistory.org/video/2057/early-muslim-expansion---conquest-of-syria-and-ira/); [World History Encyclopedia: Abbasid Revolution](https://www.worldhistory.org/video/3185/abbasid-revolution---how-the-umayyad-caliphate-fel/)
- **Hikma History** (@hikmahistory) has about 192K subscribers and averages about 14K views per video, in the Education category. A sponsorship deal was detected on 16 Dec 2025. Its 2026 uploads include "15 Battles That Shaped The Islamic World", a video on Amanullah Khan (King of Afghanistan) and "How Nationalism Ruined The Middle East". — [SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/hikmahistory)
- **Al Muqaddimah** is a one-man channel run by Syawish Rehman and named after Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddimah*. It covers mostly Islamic history, explicitly "from a liberal and secular point of view", and says a religious take is not what viewers will find there. It reportedly started partly because good videos on Islamic world history were lacking on YouTube. It makes "long & short form videos" and sells merchandise such as a *Timeline of Islamic History* poster and a *Fall of al-Andalus* poster. — [Patreon about](https://www.patreon.com/AlMuqaddimahYT/about); [YouTube Wiki (fandom)](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Al_Muqaddimah); [almuqaddimah.com](https://almuqaddimah.com/); [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@AlMuqaddimahYT)
- **"Islamic History animated"** is a small channel that tells one story a week in 10–20 minutes with stick-figure cartoons. Its tagline promises "no boring lectures". — [YouTube channel about page](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCYUoLBKMhUiss0N6Ine_RkA/about)
- **AI-generated history documentaries** are appearing. "THE ENTIRE HISTORY OF ISLAM (570–1453)" says its visuals and animations are "cinematically generated using state-of-the-art AI tools". — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UIwaJb6Ce-4&vl=en)
- Benchmark from general animated history: OverSimplified had about 9.7M subscribers but had made no new uploads since Jan 2025, according to a 2026 list. — [ThoughtLeaders, "30 Best History Channels (2026)"](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/the-best-history-channels-on-youtube)
- **1001 Inventions** is run by the UK charity Foundation for Science, Technology and Civilisation (FSTC), with Salim Al-Hassani as chief editor. It launched at London's Science Museum in 2010 and had toured 40 cities by 2021. It was still active in Nov 2025, with a special exhibition, "1001 Inventions: The Enduring Legacies of Muslim Civilisation", at the Putrajaya Festival of Ideas in Malaysia, which the Queen of Malaysia attended. — [Bernama, Nov 2025](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2488188); [AAM 2021 article (PDF)](https://www.aam-us.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/10_Exhibition_21FA_FromMuseumToRefugeeCamp.pdf)
- **Ertugrul in Urdu (2020)**: PTV aired the Urdu-dubbed *Diriliş: Ertuğrul* at PM Imran Khan's request and uploaded each episode to a dedicated Urdu YouTube channel. The channel passed 2M subscribers and 100M views in its first 16 days and had over 10M subscribers by Nov 2020. TRT put the series' global total at about 15M subscribers and over 3B views. These figures are from 2020; no 2025 figures were found. — [Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/arts/cinema/urdu-youtube-channel-of-resurrection-ertugrul-races-toward-record); [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/turkey/turkish-tv-series-urdu-youtube-channel-aims-a-record/1842698); [The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/749102-with-10-million-subscribers-ertugruls-urdu-youtube-channel-sets-new-viewership-record-in-pakistan); [Yeni Şafak](https://en.yenisafak.com/life/ertugruls-urdu-youtube-channel-tops-10mln-followers-3554957)
- **Al Jazeera English, "The Crusades: An Arab Perspective"** is a multi-episode documentary series now on YouTube. It is an example of a counter-narrative framing (the "other side's" sources). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusades,_An_Arab_Perspective)
- **Yaqeen Institute's *The Firsts*** (host Dr Omar Suleiman) is a weekly video series that "chronicles the lives of the Sahaba" during and after the Prophet's ﷺ time, one companion per episode. Examples include Adi ibn Hatim, Tamim al-Dari, Abbad ibn Bishr, Ka'b ibn Zuhayr and Jarir ibn Abdullah, plus a themed sub-arc on "the Muslims of Ta'if". No view counts were found. — [Yaqeen episode pages](https://cdn.yaqeeninstitute.org/?p=31994); [another](https://cdn.yaqeeninstitute.org/?p=31727)
- **TikTok/short-form**: a TikTok Islamic-history discovery page shows a Golden Age video (Ibn Sina, al-Zahrawi) by @abouthistory5 with about 48.6K likes. "Islam history edit" videos (music and montage) reach about 217.8K likes. A 2020 Forbes profile described @arabicmclovin using 60-second videos to "deconstruct misconceptions" and surface "forgotten parts of Muslim history". — [TikTok discover: Islamic history](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/islamic-history?lang=en); [TikTok discover: Islam history edit](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/islam-history-edit?lang=en); [Forbes 2020](https://forbes.com/sites/anharkarim/2020/10/05/how-tiktok-is-giving-us-the-muslim-representation-that-hollywood-cant/amp)
- A 2024 *Frontiers in Political Science* study of Muslim women creators on TikTok in Germany found that they position themselves as "educators and advisors" on religious topics, including Islamic history. One creator focuses on influential female historical figures. — [Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2024.1496833/full)

### Inferences
- Across successful channels, the unit of engagement is **one story or one person per episode** with a clear arc (a battle, a dynasty's fall, a companion's life), told with **maps and animation** rather than a lecturer on screen.
- Channels that cover contested early-Islamic events (Fitna, Karbala) with secular framing (Kings and Generals, Al Muqaddimah) fill a gap that religiously-trained creators leave open. A scholar-led version with sources and adab would be distinct.
- Ertugrul's Urdu success in 2020 shows huge Pakistani appetite for Islamic-history *narrative*. Most of that demand was met by drama, not by referenced history.
- AI-generated visuals are lowering production costs for history explainers. This creates both opportunity and a credibility risk, since aniconic rules and accuracy are easy to breach.

### Gaps
- Current (2025–2026) subscriber and view counts for Kings and Generals' Islamic episodes, Al Muqaddimah, and Al Jazeera Documentary's Islamic-history films were not retrievable.
- No source found on Ottoman/Turkish-language history creators (e.g. popular Turkish historians on YouTube) or on Ertugrul-era spin-off history channels in 2025–2026. Not researched due to tool limits.
- No verified list of the top "Muslim history" TikTok creators by follower count in 2025–2026.

## 2. Which projects popularise Hadith science, Qur'anic manuscripts, isnad, and research methods?

### Takeaway
Popularisation of hadith and Qur'an *scholarship* sits in a few places: Yaqeen's paired format (peer-reviewed paper plus video), academic Q&A livestreams and interviews (Marijn van Putten on manuscripts), long-running talk shows (Let the Quran Speak), and a growing layer of digital tools (Quran.com at very large scale, Usul.ai on OpenITI and Shamela, isnad-network datasets). Data visualisation of isnad is still mostly academic (papers and GitHub). No public-facing explainer brand turning these datasets into videos was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Yaqeen Institute**: its mission page lists 282 peer-reviewed papers and 2,324 videos (page date unclear). It publishes free research papers on belief, practice and contemporary questions, and has issued annual reports since 2017. — [Yaqeen mission page](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/about-us/our-mission); [Yaqeen Read](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqeen_Institute)
- Yaqeen's YouTube reach (conflicting third-party figures): more than 1.7M subscribers and 270M views per [Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Yaqeen_Institute), versus more than 2.2M subscribers and about 400M views per [ThoughtLeaders](https://app.thoughtleaders.io/youtube/yaqeen-institute). Yaqeen also has critics on the traditionalist side; see a 2020 review titled "A Source of Certainty or Doubt?" — [Muslim Skeptic (2020)](https://muslimskeptic.com/2020/04/20/yaqeen-institute-review/)
- **Usul.ai** is an AI research platform for Islamic texts built on OpenITI and al-Maktaba al-Shamela. v1 launched in Apr 2024 (search, reading, OpenITI integration) and v2 in Nov 2024 (AI, vector search, Shamela integration). It has catalogued 3,000+ texts. It is a project of the Seemorg Foundation (501(c)3 pending) and states that its AI "should not be used for requesting fatwas". — [usul.ai/about](https://usul.ai/about); [usul.ai/vision](https://usul.ai/vision)
- **Corpus Coranicum** was a Berlin-Brandenburg Academy project (2007–2024) that catalogued early Qur'an manuscripts in a database. Since 2025, M. Marx has continued it as Corpus Coranicum e.V. It has a YouTube channel. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Coranicum); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@corpuscoranicum8718)
- **Marijn van Putten** (Leiden; author of *Quranic Arabic*, Brill 2022) reaches the public through YouTube livestream Q&As on Qur'an manuscripts and Qur'anic Arabic, and through interviews such as Mythvision (2021) on oral and written transmission. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijn_van_Putten); [YouTube Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vkiji1eMGuA); [YouTube "Setting the Record Straight on Qur'anic Manuscripts"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VHPBTKrjLHk); [blog summary of Mythvision interview](https://quranicrecitations.wordpress.com/a-non-muslim-experts-view-of-the-qurans-preservation-and-the-recitations-qiraat/)
- **Let the Quran Speak** (Canada) is a weekly talk show that combines Qur'an readings, "reflections on its history and meaning" and interviews with scholars. Hosts have changed over the years: Safiyyah Ally 2006–2014, and Aisha Khaja with Dr Shabir Ally in 2018. Shabir Ally's YouTube series include "Can We Trust Hadith?". — [VisionTV](https://www.visiontv.ca/shows/let-the-quran-speak/); [Hampshire College portal](https://sites.hampshire.edu/scienceandislamvideoportal/?p=1503); [Wikipedia: Shabir Ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabir_Ally); [Playboard channel analytics](https://playboard.co/en/channel/UCaADzQEp9eYu9PTmJ28_-HQ)
- **Isnad network analysis (academic)**:
  - A social-network analysis of Sahih Muslim narrators models narrators as nodes and transmissions as edges. — [ScienceDirect 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1319157821000215)
  - The Multi-IsnadSet (MIS) dataset for Sahih Muslim (Data in Brief, 2024) was built with NetworkX, Neo4j, Gephi and Cytoscape, and released as .gml and .graphml files. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352340924004086)
  - **Muhaddithat** (Northeastern University) is a GitHub dataset of *female* hadith narrators, with Jupyter notebooks that produce graphs. — [GitHub](https://github.com/muhaddithat/isnad-datasets)
  - Isnad-parsing models (CAMeLBERT-CA, AraBART, AraT5) have been trained on Ifta' Sunnah data. This comes from a forum post and is unverified. — [Google Groups SIGARAB](https://groups.google.com/g/sigarab/c/XFnOZYvgAfg)
  - A 2025 Indonesian review claims a system called HIAS processes 15,000 hadiths in 2.3 seconds. This figure is unverified. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401434770_DIGITALISASI_DAN_ANALISIS_JARINGAN_ILMU_ISNAD_SANAD_HADIS_PEMANFAATAN_TEKNOLOGI_DIGITAL_DALAM_STUDI_KRITIK_HADIS)
- **Quran.com** (Quran Foundation) reports 63M+ active users on its web app for Mar 2024–Mar 2025, and 5M+ monthly users on its native mobile app since Jan 2025. Active users double during Ramadan. — [Quran Foundation Year in Review 2025](https://quran.foundation/year-in-review/2025); [donate page](https://donate.quran.foundation); an earlier figure of 5.6M per month comes from [Ramadan 2024 update](https://www.quran.foundation/ramadan-2024-update) (different metric)
- **The misinformation problem as a content opportunity**: an arXiv study (2025) characterises the accounts that circulate fabricated hadith on Arabic social media and notes that social media is facilitating the spread of fabricated hadith. — [arXiv 2508.07845](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07845v1)

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern is **research artefact + popular derivative**: Yaqeen's paper-plus-video, van Putten's book-plus-livestream Q&A, and Corpus Coranicum's database-plus-channel. The credibility comes from a citable source, and the reach comes from video.
- Isnad graphs, manuscript images (e.g. from Corpus Coranicum) and Quran.com/Usul-style tools are ready-made *visual raw material* for short explainers ("how this hadith reached us", "what the oldest manuscript of this surah looks like"). None of the sources found shows anyone turning them into regular, popular short videos, in any language.
- Fabricated hadith spreading widely on social media creates demand for *"is this hadith authentic?"* fact-check explainers.

### Gaps
- No usage figures found for sunnah.com, the Quranic Arabic Corpus, Shamela or Usul.ai.
- No current subscriber or view counts found for Let the Quran Speak (Playboard page not readable), Corpus Coranicum's channel, or van Putten's appearances.
- Jonathan A.C. Brown's public-facing output (lectures, books, Yaqeen papers) is well known, but no 2025–2026 source was retrieved in this session, so nothing about it is cited.
- No public-facing hadith or Qur'an data-visualisation *video* channel was found.

## 3. Which "intellectual discourse" formats exist (podcasts with academics, debates, reading clubs, book explainers, biographies, map or timeline storytelling, data journalism)?

### Takeaway
Long-form interview podcasts with academics are well established in English (Blogging Theology, Ottoman History Podcast, the Islamic History Podcast) and Arabic (Thmanyah's *Fnjan*). Biography series (*The Firsts*), timeline posters and maps (Al Muqaddimah), and serialised "answers to questions" (Ghamidi's *Response to 23 Questions*, later compiled into books) are also proven. Several formats were **not found anywhere** in 2025–2026 sources: "a classical book in 10 minutes" explainers, reading clubs on video, and data-journalism treatments of Islamic history.

### Cited Findings
- **Blogging Theology** (Paul Williams) is a YouTube channel that "attempts to introduce some academic and scholarly content" to comparative religion. It has interviewed scholars from Oxford, Cambridge, Yale, Princeton, Zaytuna and others, including Bart Ehrman. It runs topic series, such as one on the debate over the eternity of the universe, and has a spin-off *Blogging Theology Academy* co-founded with Cambridge philosopher Hassan Spiker. — [BLIIS bio](https://bliis.org/author/paul-williams/); [Ehrman Blog](https://ehrmanblog.org/a-particularly-intriguing-podcast-interview-jesus-the-bible-and-early-christianity/); [History of Philosophy podcast blog](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/blog/blogging-theology-eternity?page=4); [Yaqeen listing (2025)](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/imam-tom-live/how-islam-completed-what-christianity-began-imam-tom-facchine.md)
- **Ottoman History Podcast** has run non-commercially since 2011, with 300+ contributors (mostly academics) and about 580 episodes in English and Turkish. Its series *The Making of the Islamic World* is designed as a "university-level introduction" to Muslim history from the 7th to the 17th century, with suggested readings for each episode. — [World History Commons](https://worldhistorycommons.org/ottoman-history-podcast); [McGill Islamic Studies Library blog](https://blogs.library.mcgill.ca/islamicstudieslibrary/?p=5843)
- **The Islamic History Podcast** (founder and host Muttaqi Ismail) is an English narrative history podcast. — [Metacast episode](https://metacast.app/podcast/the-optimized-muslim-podcast---self-development/EKBmyq72/the-islamic-history-podcast---interview-with-the-founder-and-host-muttaqi-ismail/MEqjqzEr)
- **Serialised Q&A that becomes books**: Ghamidi Center of Islamic Learning (GCIL, an Al-Mawrid US initiative) runs *Response to 23 Questions*, numbered multi-part episodes (2022–2025) on Sunnah, Hadith, Itmam-e-Hujjat and the arrival of Imam Mahdi. Some parts are "dubbed with AI" into English, and GCIL is compiling the responses into topic-wise books. — [GCIL podcast feed](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/page/50/); [ghamidi.org](https://www.ghamidi.org/?p=92203)
- **Map and timeline products**: Al Muqaddimah sells timeline and al-Andalus posters alongside its videos. — [almuqaddimah.com](https://almuqaddimah.com/)
- **Weekly biography series**: Yaqeen's *The Firsts* (see Q1). — [Yaqeen](https://cdn.yaqeeninstitute.org/?p=31994)
- **Academic livestream Q&A**: van Putten's manuscript Q&As (see Q2). — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vkiji1eMGuA)
- **Long-form Arabic interview podcast at massive scale**: Thmanyah's *Fnjan* (see Q4) set a Guinness record of 110M YouTube views for one episode. — [BroadcastPro ME](https://broadcastprome.com/news/saudi-podcast-episode-breaks-record-with-110m-youtube-views)

### Inferences
- Formats that hold up are **episodic and serial** (numbered parts, weekly biographies, multi-season podcasts). Seriality builds a habit and makes a back catalogue.
- The "series → book" path (GCIL) and the "video → poster/timeline" path (Al Muqaddimah) show that research content can produce secondary products that support it.
- AI dubbing (GCIL) is already used to carry Urdu scholarship into English. The reverse direction (English or Arabic scholarship into Urdu) is an obvious, low-cost opportunity. No source was found that confirms anyone is doing it at scale.

### Gaps
- No verified examples were found of "a classical book in 10 minutes" channels (e.g. explainers of the *Muqaddimah*, *al-Risala* or *Ihya'*) or of filmed reading clubs, in any language. This may be a real gap or a search limitation.
- No Islamic-history data-journalism outlet (charts and data-driven explainers) was identified.
- Debate channels (e.g. polemics and apologetics) were not researched in this session.

## 4. Which Arabic-language channels are notable for research or history content that an Urdu scholar could adapt?

### Takeaway
The best-documented Arabic models are: **Al-Da7ee7** (Ahmed El-Ghandour; fast, comic, heavily researched monologue edutainment, now also doing Islamic-history figures such as Khalid ibn al-Walid in Dec 2025), **Al Jazeera Documentary** (high-production documentaries), **Thmanyah** (Saudi long-form podcasts and documentaries), and **Dr Raghib al-Sarjani's** referenced narrative history (*Qissat al-Andalus*, Qissat al-Islam website). Each points to a different model an Urdu scholar could adapt.

### Cited Findings
- **Al-Da7ee7 (الدحيح)**: at its first decade it was reported at "1,000 episodes and 2 billion views". It released an episode on **Khalid ibn al-Walid** in Dec 2025, which shows the edutainment format moving into Islamic-history biography. The official site lets viewers sort episodes by views. — [An-Nahar: «ألف حلقة ومليارا مشاهدة... الدحيح يحتفل بعقده الأول»](https://www.annahar.com/culture/news/223607/%D8%A3%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A9-%D9%88%D9%85%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%87%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%AD%D9%8A%D8%AD-%D9%8A%D8%AD%D8%AA%D9%81%D9%84-%D8%A8%D8%B9%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%87-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%88%D9%84) (headline only; body not readable); [Masr Times, Dec 2025](https://www.masrtimes.com/629312); [da7ee7.com episodes](https://da7ee7.com/episodes/)
- **Al Jazeera Documentary (الجزيرة الوثائقية)**: by its 15th anniversary (2021) it was reported at nearly 3M YouTube subscribers and about 2 billion minutes watched in 2021. Its programming has long included history films (e.g. on the Crusades). These are older figures. — [Al Jazeera Network page](https://network.aljazeera.net/ar/node/1211?page=1) (the source of the figures within the network site was not precisely verified); [Al Jazeera 2007 Ramadan programming](https://www.aljazeera.net/news/cultureandart/2007/9/12/رمضان-على-الجزيرة-الوثائقية-لقاء-المعرفة-بالترفيه)
- **Thmanyah (ثمانية)** was founded in 2016 (Abdulrahman Abumalih), and SRMG bought a 51% stake in Jul 2021. In 2021 it claimed 1.6M average monthly listeners and 15M viewers across podcasts and documentaries, with 90+ documentaries and short films. A *Fnjan* episode set a Guinness record of 110M YouTube views for a podcast episode; 74% of viewers were outside Saudi Arabia and 21% watched with English subtitles. — [SRMG](https://www.srmg.com/en/article/srmg-acquires-51-stake-in-podcast-platform-thmanyah-as-part-of-new-digital-first-growth-strategy); [BroadcastPro ME](https://broadcastprome.com/news/saudi-podcast-episode-breaks-record-with-110m-youtube-views); [Arab News](https://arab.news/g3q2c)
- **Dr Raghib al-Sarjani** (Egyptian preacher-historian; supervisor of the "Qissat al-Islam" website) wrote *Qissat al-Andalus min al-Fath ila al-Suqut* in two volumes, from Tariq ibn Ziyad to the fall. Readers praise it because "no detail is left without a reference" and for its periodisation. — [Abjjad reviews](https://abjjad.com/book/15456120/%D9%82%D8%B5%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%AF%D9%84%D8%B3-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%AA%D8%AD-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%82%D9%88%D8%B7/24757144/reviews); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/17455134--)
- **Shaykh Hamza Karamali / Basira**, Dr Tareq al-Suwaidan's Andalus and history series, and similar items were searched for but **not confirmed** (no results).

### Inferences
- Three adaptable Arabic models for an Urdu scholar:
  1. **Da7ee7-style**: one researched monologue per episode, fast cuts, on-screen sources, humour within adab. It is now being applied to sahaba and military history.
  2. **Al Jazeera Documentary-style**: archival and location-based short documentaries.
  3. **Thmanyah-style**: long-form conversations with experts. *Fnjan's* 74% non-Saudi audience suggests long-form Arabic intellectual content travels across borders, and subtitles widen reach.
- Al-Sarjani's referenced narrative histories, like his Andalus book, are directly usable as *source spines* for Urdu episode series, with citations shown on screen.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025–2026 subscriber counts for Da7ee7, Al Jazeera Documentary or Thmanyah.
- Could not confirm Arabic YouTube history storytellers beyond these (e.g. Tareq al-Suwaidan's history series, Ahmed al-Shugairi's heritage segments, Arabic manuscript-heritage channels). Arabic-language searches returned sparse results.
- No Arabic Islamic-manuscript popularisation channel was identified.

## 5. In Urdu specifically: who does serious research-based or history content, and what is missing?

### Takeaway
Urdu has large *preacher* audiences (Tariq Jamil ~8.8M subscribers, Tariq Masood, Raza Saqib Mustafai), a few research-style figures (Javed Ahmad Ghamidi's GCIL; Engineer Muhammad Ali Mirza, whose "book-in-hand" referencing style ended in arrest and blasphemy charges in Aug–Sep 2025), and general history storytelling (Faisal Warraich's *Dekho Suno Jano*, 1M+ subscribers). Urdu Islamic-history channels are mostly generalist and unreferenced. In 2025–2026 sources, the following were **not found** in Urdu: referenced short explainers, Qur'an manuscript stories, isnad visualisations, a regular scholar-biography series, a map-based Seerah video series (even though the Urdu *Atlas Seerat-e-Nabawi* exists in print), and respectful comparative-fiqh explainers.

### Cited Findings
- **Faisal Warraich, *Dekho Suno Jano***: a podcast promo calls it "Pakistan's most popular YouTube channel on Politics and History" (self-promotional). A YouTube description says it has over 1M subscribers. Topics include the history of Pakistan (Partition to the Afghan jihad), the Ottoman Empire and US history. — [Apple Podcasts (The Pakistan Experience #286)](https://podcasts.apple.com/cy/podcast/jinnah-nehru-and-the-great-game-of-partition/id1475013650?i=1000624253330); [YouTube "History Of Pakistan"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V5BX9SVUBgk); [X profile](https://x.com/fswarraich?lang=en); [360newschannel profile](https://360newschannel.com/faisal-warraich-journey-from-youtube-to-sucess/)

### Inferences
Gaps in Urdu, relative to the English and Arabic models found:
1. **Referenced short explainers** (60–90 s) with the ayah or hadith number, the book and the grading shown on screen. No Urdu equivalent of Yaqeen's paper-plus-video was found.
2. **Qur'an manuscript and preservation stories**, Corpus Coranicum or van Putten-style but from a believing scholar. Nothing found in Urdu.
3. **Isnad and "how this hadith reached us" visual explainers**. The datasets exist (MIS, Muhaddithat), but no Urdu or South Asian use was found.
4. **A weekly scholar or companion biography series** (a *The Firsts* equivalent), including *South Asian* ulama such as Shah Waliullah and the muhaddithin of the subcontinent. Not found as a regular Urdu series.
5. **A map-based Seerah video series**. The Urdu atlas exists in print, but no video adaptation was found.
6. **Respectful comparative fiqh**. The Urdu space is polarised along sectarian lines (sectarian TV channels). The Mirza case shows legal risk when comparative or critical remarks go viral in Pakistan. This argues for a "what the madhahib say, with sources and adab" format that names no opponents.
7. **Turning Arabic and English scholarship into Urdu** (subtitled or AI-dubbed explainers based on Arabic sources such as al-Sarjani's histories, with on-screen citation). GCIL already shows the reverse direction (Urdu to English).

On the competitive landscape: Urdu *history* storytelling (Warraich) and Urdu *preaching* (Tariq Jamil, Tariq Masood) are both crowded. The intersection, a **scholar who tells referenced Islamic history and hadith stories with maps and visuals**, appears thin. Faisal Warraich covers history but not hadith or fiqh scholarship. Ghamidi covers scholarship but in lecture or interview form, not visual storytelling.

## 6. What evidence exists on engagement for long-form educational versus short-form content in this niche?

### Takeaway
The evidence is mixed and mostly small-sample. The largest Islam-specific study (73,120 Islamic YouTube videos, 2011–2020) found **no significant relationship between video length and engagement**. A cross-religion study reports that **long videos get about 2.2 times the views and more comments, while shorts get more likes**. Smaller Indonesian studies (2025–2026) report 3–5 times higher engagement rates for 15–60 s clips and warn that "popularity outweighs depth". Very large long-form successes also exist (*Fnjan* 110M views; Yaqeen at hundreds of millions of views). Both formats work, but for different metrics.

### Cited Findings
- **Heliyon (2022), "Social mediatization of religion: Islamic videos on YouTube"**: a quantitative study of **73,120 Islamic YouTube videos (2011–2020)** found that users' engagement "is not related to the length of Islamic videos in any significant way". The authors attribute this to positive and supportive attitudes towards online Islamic videos. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844022003711); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8919218/)
- **Cross-religion study (about 1,100 videos; Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism)**:
  - Regular videos generated **2.19 times more views** than short-form videos.
  - Short-form videos received **1.55 times higher engagement in likes**.
  - Regular videos received **1.24 times more comments**.
  - Caveat: these figures came from a search extract. Their attribution to the "Digital Landscape of God" paper is likely but not confirmed in full text. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395527377_The_Digital_Landscape_of_God_Narrative_Visuals_and_Viewer_Engagement_of_Religious_Videos_on_YouTube)
- **"The Digital Landscape of God" (ICWSM 2026; arXiv Sep 2025)**:
  - Religious videos "predominantly adopt speaking-style formats with authority-based persuasion".
  - It reports significant correlations between content features and engagement, "particularly regarding AI-generated content".
  - It cites a Pew figure that 20% watch religious videos on video-sharing platforms.
  - TikTok #religion views rose from 2M to over 2B in two years. — [arXiv 2509.10957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10957v1); [ICWSM/AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42649)
- **Al-Qalam (2025), "The Transformation of Religious Practices of Digital Ummah in the Age of Algorithms"**:
  - Data: the top 30 viral religious videos on each of TikTok, YouTube and Instagram over one month, plus 5 ulama and 5 influencer interviews.
  - 15–60 s clips had a **3–5 times higher engagement rate** than long-form.
  - Dominant themes were prohibiting heresy (32%), criticising modern lifestyles (25%) and practical worship guides (18%).
  - The authors conclude that "digital popularity outweighs the depth of knowledge".
  - Caveat: small sample. — [Al-Qalam journal](https://jurnalalqalam.or.id/index.php/Alqalam/article/view/1702); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397576428_THE_TRANSFORMATION_OF_RELIGIOUS_PRACTICES_OF_DIGITAL_UMMAH_IN_THE_AGE_OF_ALGORITHMS)
- A 2026 synthesis in *Kalijaga Journal of Communication* finds religious authority shifting from institutions to da'wah influencers, with risk of misinformation "due to the lack of formal verification mechanisms". — [Kalijaga J. Comm.](https://ejournal.uin-suka.ac.id/dakwah/kjc/article/view/3801)
- A study of hadith content across platforms reports that religious influencers dominate over traditionally trained scholars. — [Living Hadith journal](https://ejournal.uin-suka.ac.id/ushuluddin/Living/article/view/7745)
- Another Indonesian study of 200 top-performing posts reports that the highest interaction (an 85% engagement rate) correlates with "technical mastery of micro-duration content (30–60 seconds)", not theological depth. The attribution of this to a specific paper is uncertain; it is most likely the ICRESH proceedings. — [ICRESH proceedings](https://proceedings.uinmybatusangkar.ac.id/index.php/icresh/article/view/132)
- **Long-form at scale (counter-evidence)**:
  - *Fnjan* (Thmanyah) episode: 110M YouTube views. — [BroadcastPro ME](https://broadcastprome.com/news/saudi-podcast-episode-breaks-record-with-110m-youtube-views)
  - Yaqeen: about 270–400M lifetime YouTube views (conflicting trackers). — [ThoughtLeaders](https://app.thoughtleaders.io/youtube/yaqeen-institute)
  - Al Jazeera Documentary: about 2B minutes watched in 2021. — [Al Jazeera Network](https://network.aljazeera.net/ar/node/1211?page=1)
- **Mid-size history channel benchmark**: Hikma History averages about 14K views per video on about 192K subscribers, and earns sponsorship income. — [SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/hikmahistory)
- **Seasonality**: Quran.com active users double in Ramadan. — [Quran Foundation](https://donate.quran.foundation)

### Inferences
- **Short-form** wins on likes and engagement *rate* and drives discovery. **Long-form** wins on views per video, comments (discussion) and watch-time, which matters for YouTube recommendations and sponsorship.
- In this niche the evidence supports a **hybrid funnel**: referenced 60–90 s Reels or TikToks that point to a long-form episode or source.
- The finding that popularity beats depth means a research-led Urdu creator must win on *packaging* (hook, visuals, pace) while keeping the depth. Indonesian evidence suggests viral religious short-form is dominated by polemic ("prohibiting heresy" 32%). A non-polemical, referenced short could stand out, though it is not guaranteed to get the same reach.
- Ramadan is a predictable spike in demand for Qur'an and Seerah content.

### Gaps
- No Urdu- or Pakistan-specific quantitative study comparing short-form and long-form engagement for Islamic content was found.
- The full texts of the cross-religion and Al-Qalam studies could not be read, so their methods (e.g. how engagement rate was normalised) are unverified.
- The general YouTube Shorts vs regular videos paper ([arXiv 2403.00454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.00454)) was identified, but its findings could not be retrieved.
- No platform-level data (Facebook Reels or TikTok in Pakistan) on religious-history content performance was found.
