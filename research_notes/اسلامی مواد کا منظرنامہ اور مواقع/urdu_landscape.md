# Urdu-Language Islamic Scholars and Da'wah Creators Online: Landscape 2025–2026

Research note scope: Pakistani and diaspora Urdu-speaking Islamic voices on YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X and podcasts. Research date: 22 Oct 2026.

Method caveat for the report writer: subscriber counts below come mostly from third-party trackers (Social Blade, vidIQ, SocialCounts, Qoruz) and news reports, captured on different dates. Direct page fetches of Social Blade, vidIQ, Dawn and Wikipedia failed during this session (DNS errors), so many figures are taken from search-result snippets of those pages and could not be opened and checked directly. Treat all counts as approximate and always quote the date.

---

## 1. Who are the largest and fastest-growing Urdu Islamic channels/voices, and how big are they?

### Takeaway
Maulana Tariq Jamil is the largest single Urdu Islamic YouTube voice (about 8.8M on his main channel, Oct 2026). Next come the Mufti Tariq Masood channel cluster (about 5M on the largest channel, plus several official and fan channels in the 1–2M range), Engineer Muhammad Ali Mirza (about 3.1M, Aug 2025), Dr Israr Ahmad's legacy channels (about 1.9M, Feb 2025; about 3M before a 2022 takedown), Raza Saqib Mustafai (about 1.4M, undated) and Dr Tahir-ul-Qadri (about 0.9M, Jul 2026). Ghamidi's official channels are smaller by subscribers (about 364K for GCIL) but very large by video count. Audiences are split across many official, semi-official and fan channels, so no single number captures any one scholar's reach.

## 2. What formats does each use?

### Takeaway
The dominant format is still the recorded long bayan or lecture, cut into many short clips. Q&A ("masail") is the second pillar. Podcasts (scholars as guests on influencer podcasts, or running their own) and seminary media-house productions (series, nasheeds, vlogs, animated explainers) are the newer layer.

### Cited Findings
- **Tariq Masood:** his clip channels upload "Islamic Videos for Daily Basis & Latest Bayanat and Short Clips". Separate "Ask" (Q&A) and "Speeches" channels exist. — [vidIQ / Islamic Bayan description](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCP1HZK52Rfp7rVzXWphH30g); [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/askmuftitariqmasood)
- **Tariq Masood (academic finding):** a 2024 study of his YouTube sermons and podcasts found that his communicative style contributes to his popularity among youth. — [Wikipedia: Tariq Masood (citing 2024 study)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_Masood)
- **JTR Media House:** formats include a podcast playlist, "Islahi Majalis", Dars-e-Hadith, Tafseer-e-Quran, "JTR Kalams and Nasheeds", "JTR Vlogs" and a Q&A show, "Aap Kay Masail ka Hal". The show is also cut into clips such as "Qabar Par Phool Dalna?". These are distributed on Hidayah TV as well. — [Hidayah TV](https://hidayah.tv/); [Hidayah TV clip page](https://hidayah.tv/shows/aap-kay-masail-ka-hal/qabar-par-phool-dalna-jamia-tur-rasheed/2574); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCkHaPNQVjF4Qjobcc2Mrevw/videos)
- **Ghamidi / GCIL:** formats include short topical talks by Hassan Ilyas (e.g. "Shadi Say Pehlay Chand Aham Faislay", about 10 minutes, with volunteer English subtitles), an animated explainer series on Quranic inheritance law (Part 3 dubbed into English, 27 Aug 2025), and long numbered Q&A series ("Response to 23 Questions", Part 68+). Full-Quran series ("Quran Urdu Translation", "Al-Bayan" lectures) are mirrored as a Podbean podcast. — [Amara (Hassan Ilyas)](https://amara.org/th/videos/XIsaUNEcurle/info/shadi-say-pehlay-chand-aham-faislay-muhammad-hassan-ilyas/); [GCIL Podbean](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/page/78/); [Amara (23 Questions)](https://amara.org/th/videos/cU9BrFWP9m0F/info/response-to-23-questions-part-68-excommunication-from-islam-takfeer-javed-ahmed-ghamidi/); [Podbean (Surah Sad, Jun 2022)](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/e/quran-urdu-translation-part-38-surah-sad-javed-ahmed-ghamidi)
- **Hassan Ilyas** is presented in GCIL descriptions as a young theologian and Arabic-literature researcher, a close associate of Ghamidi and part of his hadith research project. This makes him a second-generation on-camera face for the institution. — [GCIL Podbean](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/page/22/)
- **Engineer Mirza:** the August 2025 case arose from an interview that was also uploaded to his own YouTube channel. Interviews and podcasts are therefore part of his output alongside his academy lectures. — [The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1339183-cleric-engineer-muhammad-ali-mirza-arrested-sent-to-jail)
- **Minhaj-ul-Quran:** runs a dedicated app (Minhaj TV) with live streaming and an archive of speeches back to 1982, an institutional TV/OTT model. — [Minhaj.tv](https://www.minhaj.tv/english/News-Events/page/11/)
- **Sahil Adeem:** his formats are his own audio/video podcast (Acast), live tours (UK 2025) and appearances on other podcasts and digital talk shows: Samaa Digital's "Makalma", EON podcast, The Current Podcast, TBT Digital, and a debate with Ismaeel Qasim. — [Acast](https://shows.acast.com/sahil-adeem-podcast/episodes/sahil-adeem-in-london-uk-tour-2025-sahil-adeem-podcast); [Aaj News](https://english.aaj.tv/news/amp/330367746); [YouTube debate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dy33CIuAe70)
- **General-interest podcasts:** the "Hafiz Ahmed Podcast" (host described as an e-commerce entrepreneur) mixes celebrity guests (writer Khalil-ur-Rehman Qamar, actress Nadia Khan) with religious guests such as Hafiz Atif Waheed, son of Dr Israr Ahmad, who discussed Markazi Anjuman Khuddam-ul-Quran. — [Brandsynario](https://www.brandsynario.com/entertainment/khalil-ur-rehman-refuses-to-work-with-saba-qamar-over-revealing-clothes/); [Bol News](https://www.bolnews.com/?p=793144); [Rephonic listing](https://rephonic.com/podcasts/ta-talks)
- **Zakir Naik:** uses live public lecture tours with Q&A (Pakistan, Oct 2024) as content events. — [WION](https://www.wionews.com/south-asia/fugitive-islamic-preacher-zakir-naik-meets-pak-pm-all-set-for-interactions-across-country-763949/amp)

### Inferences
- The "speech channel + Q&A channel + clip channel" triad (seen around Tariq Masood) is a repeatable structure: long content for loyal viewers, Q&A for search, clips for algorithmic reach.
- Seminary-owned media houses (JTR, Minhaj TV, GCIL) are the closest Urdu analogues to professional studios. They run formal series, Q&A shows and nasheeds/kalams, and some (GCIL) add animated explainers and dubbing.

### Gaps
- No verified information on Tariq Jamil's current format mix (e.g. share of shorts versus full bayans), Raja Zia ul Haq's formats, or Mufti Taqi Usmani's video output beyond the Darse Quran/Jumu'ah podcast.
- No sources found on street da'wah, debate shows or audiobooks among Urdu creators in 2025–26.

## 3. What editing and visual styles dominate? Which channels are known for distinctive production?

### Takeaway
I found almost no published analysis of Urdu da'wah editing styles, so this question is largely unanswered by sources. The only evidence is indirect: animated and dubbed explainers (GCIL), seminary nasheed/kalam and vlog production (JTR), and a CapCut template ecosystem for remixing Dr Israr Ahmad audio.

### Cited Findings
- GCIL produces an animated explainer series (inheritance law, Part 3 dubbed into English, Aug 2025). — [GCIL Podbean](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/page/22/)
- JTR Media House presents itself as a "video production house", with separate output lines for kalams/nasheeds, vlogs and podcasts. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/c/JTRMediaHouse); [Hidayah TV](https://hidayah.tv/)
- CapCut runs a dedicated explore/template page for "doctor israr ahmed" edits, which points to fan-made short edits (typically audio over visuals) rather than official production. — [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/explore/doctor-israr-ahmed)
- A review paper on Islamic preaching for Pakistani users argues that traditionally trained scholars often carry their classroom or minbar style unchanged onto YouTube. — [ResearchGate (2023)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374232427_Islamic_Preaching_Using_Digital_Media_to_Approach_Pakistani_Users)

### Inferences
- Because most top channels publish raw lecture footage, a well-produced Urdu talking-head reel (captions, clean audio, an aniconic background, reference cards) is likely to stand out. This is an inference from the "classroom style carried to YouTube" finding, not a measured result.
- JTR's reputation for higher production quality, which the brief cites, is plausible from its media-house structure, but no source in this session describes its grading, camera work or music policy.

### Gaps
- No sources found on caption styles, B-roll, colour grading, thumbnail and hook conventions, nasheed versus no-music policies, or AI-generated visuals in Urdu Islamic content.
- JTR Media House's specific visual signature (cinematic grading, drone shots and so on) could not be verified from any source.
- The Dawn profile of Tariq Jamil, which may describe his presentation style and media team, could not be fetched.

## 4. How are series structured, and how often do they post?

### Takeaway
Series are numbered and organised around texts: Quran translation or tafsir by surah, Q&A responses by part number, and Dars-e-Hadith. Q&A shows are cut into question-level clips. Posting frequency is high for archive and clip channels (thousands of videos), but I found no verified per-week cadence.

### Cited Findings
- GCIL: "Quran Urdu Translation" is published as numbered parts by surah (e.g. Part 11 Surah Hud, Part 38 Surah Sad, Jun 2022), alongside "Al-Bayan" lectures and "Response to 23 Questions" (Part 68). — [Podbean Hud](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/e/quran-urdu-translation-part-11-surah-hud-javed-ahmed-ghamidi); [Podbean Sad](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/e/quran-urdu-translation-part-38-surah-sad-javed-ahmed-ghamidi); [Podbean Al-Bayan](https://www.podbean.com/media/share/pb-3jgbk-12a6e6a); [Amara](https://amara.org/th/videos/cU9BrFWP9m0F/info/response-to-23-questions-part-68-excommunication-from-islam-takfeer-javed-ahmed-ghamidi/)
- GCIL's podcast feed runs to at least 90 pages of episodes, reflecting a very high volume. — [GCIL Podbean page 90](https://ghamidicil.podbean.com/page/90/)
- Video counts as a proxy for output: GCIL 10,717 videos; Ghamidi Official 4,643; Tahir-ul-Qadri 6,530; @muftitariqmasood about 6.3K; Mirza about 2,400 (Aug 2025). — [SocialCounts GCIL](https://socialcounts.org/youtube-live-subscriber-count/UCd0F5R_zECxivBm7bQoWLVQ); [SocialCounts Ghamidi](https://socialcounts.org/youtube-live-subscriber-count/UC1isGGyqPXxGb3i_Tr6agHg); [SocialCounts Qadri](https://socialcounts.org/youtube-live-subscriber-count/UC3TenRnIEWa-P-JliJv3yTQ); [Social Blade TM](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/muftitariqmasood/achievements); [The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1339183-cleric-engineer-muhammad-ali-mirza-arrested-sent-to-jail)
- JTR: the Q&A show "Aap Kay Masail ka Hal" is published both as full shows and as single-question clips. Named series include "Islahi Majalis", "Dars e Hadith" and "Tafseer e Quran". — [Hidayah TV](https://hidayah.tv/shows/aap-kay-masail-ka-hal/qabar-par-phool-dalna-jamia-tur-rasheed/2574)
- The unofficial Tariq Masood clip channel states it uploads on a "daily basis". — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCP1HZK52Rfp7rVzXWphH30g)
- Mufti Taqi Usmani's Darse Quran and Jumu'ah khutbat are distributed as a podcast series, implying weekly Friday-linked content. — [Rephonic](https://rephonic.com/podcasts/darse-quran-by-mufti-taqi-usmani-jummah-khutbat)

### Inferences
- The proven Urdu series model is: (a) a text-anchored long series (by surah or hadith-book chapter) for depth and archive, and (b) a Q&A show that is clipped by question for search and short-form discovery.
- High video counts (thousands) reflect institutional archiving of every dars, not necessarily a planned editorial calendar.

### Gaps
- No verified uploads-per-week figures for any channel in 2025–26.
- No information on Seerah series structures (e.g. Tariq Jamil's or Raja Zia ul Haq's) from this session.

## 5. What growth tactics are visible?

### Takeaway
Visible growth levers are (1) fan or "not official" clip channels and template edits that repurpose lectures, (2) appearances on influencer podcasts and digital talk shows, (3) live tours, including diaspora tours, (4) multi-language subtitles and dubbing, and (5) controversy, which brings attention but also legal risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Clip farms:** unofficial channels such as "Islamic Bayan" (about 724–741K subscribers) and "Islam Tube Urdu" openly repost Tariq Masood bayans and short clips with "not official" disclaimers. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCP1HZK52Rfp7rVzXWphH30g); [Qoruz](https://qoruz.com/islamicbayanmtm/youtube)
- **Template edits:** CapCut hosts template and explore pages for Dr Israr Ahmad edits. — [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/explore/doctor-israr-ahmed)
- **Podcast cross-over:** religious figures appear on general-interest Pakistani podcasts, for example Hafiz Atif Waheed (Dr Israr's son) on the Hafiz Ahmed Podcast. ## 6. What audience complaints or gaps are visible?

### Takeaway
Documented concerns are: sectarian and hate content (particularly against Ahmadis and across sects), scepticism toward "celebrity" or self-proclaimed scholars, a ritual-focused classroom style that misses everyday issues, mixed effects on youth tolerance, and the shallowness of short-form formats. Specific evidence on unverified hadith or missing references in Urdu content was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Youth focus-group study:** a study of Pakistani undergraduates aged 20–26 (Bano & Hafeez) found mixed effects. Some reported stronger faith and tolerance; others showed exclusionary attitudes and rigid readings. Recurring themes were peer influence, algorithmic exposure and **scepticism toward celebrity scholars**. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396849472_The_Digitalization_of_Islam_on_YouTube_and_Pakistani_Youth_Religious_Identity_Belief_and_Tolerance_in_a_Digital_Age)
- **Style mismatch:** traditionally trained scholars bring classroom styles to YouTube and focus on rituals rather than everyday issues. — [ResearchGate (2023)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374232427_Islamic_Preaching_Using_Digital_Media_to_Approach_Pakistani_Users)
- **Wider reach, mixed results:** social media widens scholars' reach but also has unfavourable effects on da'wah (Keerio et al.). — [ResearchGate (2024)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379268118_Understanding_the_Impact_of_Promoting_Da'wah_Using_Social_Media_Platforms_Among_Pakistani_Muslims)
- **Self-proclaimed scholars:** Dr Tahir-ul-Qadri told youth to "stay away from self-proclaimed scholars on social media". — [Minhaj.tv](https://minhaj.tv/english/tid/60695/Youth-Should-Stay-Away-from-Self-Proclaimed-Scholars-on-Social-Media-Dr-Tahir-ul-Qadri.html)
- **Short-form concern:** a 2025 Karachi-based journal article notes that TikTok's short format has drawn millions of young consumers in Islamic societies, and that scholars and religious leaders have voiced concern about it. — [Insights Journal](https://insights-journal.com/index.php/insights/article/download/39/16/200)
- **Sectarian and hate content:** clerics and madrassas have used Facebook pages and YouTube channels for propaganda against Ahmadis (Diplomat, Jul 2021). The US State Department's 2023 report notes a Pakistani ministry call for awareness among religious scholars to curb misuse of social media, and cites 3,181 URLs blocked for sectarian or hate-speech content. — [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2021/07/pakistans-social-media-is-overflowing-with-hate-speech-against-ahmadis/); [US State Dept 2023 IRF Report](https://2021-2025.state.gov/reports/2023-report-on-international-religious-freedom/pakistan/)
- **Administrative speech bans:** during Muharram 2024, the Jhelum DC banned speeches by 17 clerics, including Mirza. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Mirza)
- **Platform enforcement:** YouTube removed Dr Israr Ahmad's about-3M channel in 2022 over alleged antisemitic content. — [The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/print/947134-youtube-takes-down-dr-israr-s-channel-for-anti-jews-remarks)
- **Misogyny complaints** against Sahil Adeem (2024) and Zakir Naik (2024 tour). — [Aaj News](https://english.aaj.tv/news/amp/330367746); [Sabrang India](https://sabrangindia.in/zakir-naiks-lecture-tour-sparks-outrage-in-pakistan-misogyny-and-extremism-in-the-spotlight)
- **Debate over substance:** a debate titled "Sahil Adeem is All Talk? No Practical Solution?" shows audience pushback against motivational-style religious talk. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dy33CIuAe70)

### Inferences
- There is a visible opening for Urdu content that is (a) clearly referenced (ayah and hadith source on screen), (b) non-polemical and non-sectarian, (c) about daily-life issues (family, work, akhlaq), and (d) delivered by a recognisable aalim rather than a "celebrity" speaker. This fits the scepticism and style-mismatch findings above, but it is an inference, not a measured audience demand.
- The legal and platform risks (PECA, 295-C, MPO, YouTube takedowns) mean that avoiding sectarian or polemical clips is also a risk-management choice.

### Gaps
- No sources found that systematically document unverified or weak hadith, missing references or clickbait thumbnails in Urdu Islamic content. These complaints are common in forums, but no Reddit or forum threads were successfully retrieved in this session.
- No audience-survey data (e.g. which formats Urdu viewers prefer, or demand for academic depth) was found.
- No data was found on diaspora (UK, Gulf, North America) Urdu audience preferences specifically.
