# Research integrity

The user's reputation is the asset. Everything here protects it. (Policies change: confirm the current
HEC policy and the target journal's rules before each submission.)

## Plagiarism and similarity

- HEC's plagiarism policy is applied through Turnitin. The commonly applied limits: **overall similarity
  ≤ 19%**, and **no single source above ~5%** (excluding the bibliography and properly quoted text, per the
  checker's settings). Many journals set a stricter limit (often 10–15%). Check the journal.
- Quotations must be in quotation marks / block quotes with a reference; paraphrase must be a real
  re-expression plus a reference.
- Similarity is not the same as plagiarism: correctly quoted Quran/Hadith and standard phrases may match.
  Explain these to the editor if needed.
- Get a real Turnitin report via the university library, supervisor or ORIC before submitting.

## Self-plagiarism (the thesis)

- The thesis text is probably in Turnitin's repository. Re-using its sentences = high similarity.
- Rewrite fresh; cite the thesis; disclose that the article is derived from it (see `thesis-to-article.md`).
- `rtools.py overlap draft.md thesis.md`: target **< 5% of the draft's 8-word sequences** found in the
  thesis, and no long matching passage except quotations of primary sources.
- Do not publish the same findings twice (duplicate publication) or split one finding into many papers.

## AI use (honest and safe)

- Journals (Elsevier, Springer, Taylor & Francis, Sage, MDPI, and increasingly HEC journals) follow COPE:
  AI cannot be an author; authors are responsible for every word; **AI use must be disclosed** (usually in
  the methods or acknowledgements), and fabricated citations are misconduct.
- How this skill keeps the work genuinely the user's:
  - the research question, reading, judgements and conclusions come from the user;
  - drafting is collaborative: Claude proposes structure, outlines and draft text, the user rewrites and
    approves in their own voice;
  - all sources are real and checked;
  - the final text is specific, argued and personal, which is what distinguishes scholarship from generic
    machine text.
- Never use "AI humaniser", paraphrase-spinning, or invisible-character tricks to beat detectors. They are
  detectable, they damage the prose, and if discovered they look like intent to deceive.
- AI detectors are unreliable; the protection is real authorship plus a record of the process
  (notes, drafts, `log.md`). Keep the drafts.
- Suggested disclosure (adapt to the journal):
  > "The author used an AI assistant (Claude, Anthropic) for language editing and for organising notes.
  > All sources were consulted and verified by the author, who takes full responsibility for the content."

## Authorship and ethics

- Co-authors only for real contribution (supervisor commonly co-authors thesis papers — agree in advance).
- Empirical work with people: informed consent, anonymity, and ethics approval (university ERB / ORIC).
- Avoid predatory journals and paid "guaranteed publication"; check HJRS and Scopus / WoS lists directly.
- One journal at a time: never submit the same paper to two journals simultaneously.
